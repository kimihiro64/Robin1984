\begin{abstract}
For a number field \(K\), we prove that the Riemann hypothesis for
\(\zeta_K\) is equivalent to an eventual bound for the normalized
sum of the norms of the divisors of an integral ideal. The bound has
main term \(e^\gamma\kappa_K\log\log N\mathfrak a\) and correction
\(e^\gamma\kappa_K(12+3\log|D_K|)/\sqrt{\log N\mathfrak a}\),
where \(\kappa_K\) is the zeta residue at \(1\).
Cyclotomic factorization gives the equivalent bound for Dirichlet
GRH, and the all-field quantifier gives Dedekind ERH.
For the standard \(L\)-functions of unitary cuspidal representations
of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) over all number fields, the critical-line
assertion is equivalent to a one-sided square-root bound for a
triangular sum over the prime powers of a representation and its
contragredient. The converse in both settings uses a Mellin
transform, Landau's theorem, and the sign of the residue at a
possible real zero.

Lean verifies both corrected ideal equivalences, as well as the
classical Robin, colossally abundant, and Lagarias statements.
It verifies the triangular criterion for an indexed family with
the stated standard analytic properties and constructs the
standard rank-one family over every number field. The
higher-rank analytic inputs in the paper come from the classical
theorems on standard \(L\)-functions. Their instantiation in Lean
remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Robin's criterion detects the Riemann hypothesis through the sizes of
divisor sums~\cite{Robin1984}. We extend the divisor-sum problem to
integral ideals of a number field \(K\), using the absolute ideal norm
and the residue of \(\zeta_K\) at \(1\). A logarithmic inequality for
each local divisor factor allows us to charge the cost of a prime
power against a single norm budget. This cancels the boundary term in
the number-field Mertens formula. In the converse direction, an ideal
formed from all prime powers up to a given norm recovers the same
weighted prime-ideal error. The resulting fieldwise equivalence gives
the Dirichlet GRH criterion on cyclotomic fields and the Dedekind ERH
criterion on all number fields.

For the standard \(L\)-functions of unitary cuspidal automorphic
representations, conjugation makes the paired logarithmic coefficients
real, and their signs can vary. We use these coefficients in a
triangular Riesz sum over prime powers. The same one-sided Mellin
argument applies to its remainder and to the divisor-sum error.
The key real poles have a positive residue before subtraction from a
nonnegative comparison function. This sign also rules out a real
exceptional zero in the relevant half-plane.

The argument draws on number-field Mertens theorems
\cite{GarciaLeeNumberFieldMertens}, on smoothed explicit formulas for prime
ideals~\cite{GrenieMolteniSmoothed}, and on Landau's theorem for
nonnegative transforms~\cite{Landau1905}. The triangular sum for
\(\zeta_K\) is the first Riesz mean used by Greni\'e and Molteni,
divided by \(x\). Suzuki's sign criteria use logarithmically weighted
von Mangoldt sums for \(\zeta\) and Dirichlet \(L\)-functions
\cite[Theorems~1, 3 and~6]{SuzukiChebyshev}. Garg and Maji obtain
criteria for a class of \(L\)-functions, including full-level
holomorphic Hecke eigenforms, using exponential weights on
Dirichlet inverse coefficients~\cite[Theorem~3.10 and
Corollary~3.12]{GargMajiGrand}. Lagarias's criterion for standard
\(L\)-functions uses Li coefficients~\cite{LagariasAutomorphicLi}.
Our criteria concern the eventual ideal-divisor bound with its explicit
correction and the one-sided \(O(\sqrt{x})\) bound for the triangular
prime-power remainder of paired standard factors in every rank.
The Alp\"oge--Claude development gives the classical and Dirichlet
critical-line statements; its release and independent verification
record specify those quantifiers~\cite{Alpoge2026,Sutherland2026}.
Slijep\v{c}evi\'c's outline of that argument describes a related
one-sided Riesz criterion for Dirichlet zeros. It uses a positive
combination of twisted sums with coefficients \(\Lambda(n)/n\) and
requires bounds on windows \([Y,Y^\lambda]\) for arbitrarily large
\(Y\)~\cite[Eq.~(2.3) and Proposition~2.2]{SlijepcevicAlpogeOutline}.
The triangular criterion in Section~\ref{sec:grand-research} uses
the coefficients of \(-Z'/Z\) directly and requires the square-root
bound at all sufficiently large \(x\).

The main arguments are given in Sections~\ref{sec:positive-mellin}--%
\ref{sec:grand-research}. Appendix~\ref{app:classical-criteria} records
the exact classical Robin cutoff, the colossally abundant reduction,
and the proof reconstruction. The Lagarias criterion and the
verification artifacts have their own appendices.

\section{A one-sided Mellin criterion}\label{sec:positive-mellin}

For a real, locally integrable function \(U\) on \([1,\infty)\), write
\[
 \mathcal{M}U(s)=\int_1^\infty U(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx
\]
where the integral converges absolutely.

\begin{lemma}[Landau's theorem and the sign of a residue]
\label{lem:one-sided-mellin}
Suppose \(\mathcal{M}U\) converges absolutely in some right half-plane
and extends meromorphically to \(\Re s>\alpha\), with every real pole
there simple and of positive residue. If \(U(x)\le Cx^\alpha\) for
all sufficiently large \(x\), then \(\mathcal{M}U\) has no pole in
\(\Re s>\alpha\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(X>1\) beyond the cutoff and consider
\[
 G(x)=\mathbf{1}_{[X,\infty)}(x)(Cx^\alpha-U(x))\geq0.
\]
Its transform continues meromorphically to \(\Re s>\alpha\) as
\[
 \mathcal{M}G(s)=\frac{C X^{\alpha-s}}{s-\alpha}
       -\mathcal{M}U(s)+\int_1^X U(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx.
\]
The integral over the compact interval is entire. If a pole of
\(\mathcal{M}U\) lay to the right of \(\alpha\), the abscissa of
convergence \(\sigma_c\) of the transform of \(G\) would be at least
the real part of that pole. Indeed, the convergent transform is
holomorphic to the right of \(\sigma_c\), and agrees there with the
meromorphic continuation by uniqueness. Now \(\sigma_c>\alpha\).
Landau's theorem~\cite{Landau1905}, applied to the Laplace transform
after \(x=e^u\), forces a singularity at the real point \(\sigma_c\).
The continuation can only have a simple pole there and its residue is
negative. As \(s\downarrow\sigma_c\) on the real axis the transform
would tend to \(-\infty\), contradicting its nonnegative integral.
\end{proof}

For the inversions below, it suffices to compare absolutely convergent
transforms on \(\Re s=2\). Under \(x=e^u\) those transforms are
Fourier transforms of \(L^1\) functions on \(u\geq0\). Uniqueness
gives equality almost everywhere, which gives pointwise equality
whenever the two expressions are continuous.

\section{The corrected ideal Robin criterion}\label{sec:grh}

Let \(K\) be a number field with discriminant \(D_K\), ring of
integers \(\mathcal O_K\), and residue
\(\kappa_K=\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)>0\).
We use \(N\) for the absolute ideal norm and \(\gamma\) for the
Euler--Mascheroni constant. For a nonzero integral ideal
\(\mathfrak a\), put
\[
 \sigma_K(\mathfrak a)=\sum_{\mathfrak d\mid\mathfrak a}N\mathfrak d,
\]
where all divisors are integral ideals. A statement about sufficiently
large \(N\mathfrak a\) allows a threshold depending on \(K\).

\begin{theorem}[Fieldwise corrected ideal Robin criterion]
\label{thm:ideal-robin}
All zeros of \(\zeta_K(s)\) in \(0<\Re s<1\) have real part \(1/2\)
if and only if, for every \(\mathfrak a\) of sufficiently large norm,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-robin}
 \frac{\sigma_K(\mathfrak a)}{N\mathfrak a}
 \leq e^\gamma\kappa_K
 \left(\log\log N\mathfrak a+
           \frac{12+3\log|D_K|}{\sqrt{\log N\mathfrak a}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The boundary term in Mertens' formula}

In this section \(q=N\mathfrak p\), with \(\mathfrak p\) a nonzero
prime ideal. Every prime-power sum is over pairs
\((\mathfrak p,j)\), \(j\geq1\), counted separately even when their
norms coincide. Define
\[
 \psi_K(x)=\sum_{q^j\leq x}\log q,\qquad
 E(x)=\psi_K(x)-x,\qquad
 A(x)=\sum_{q^j\leq x}\frac{1}{jq^j}.
\]
For \(x>1\) let \(w(x)=(1+\log x)/\log^2 x\), and set
\[
 H(x)=-\int_x^\infty E(t)\frac{w(t)}{t^2}\,dt,\qquad
 H_0(x)=-\int_x^\infty E(t)\frac{dt}{t^2}.
\]
The fixed-field prime ideal theorem gives
\(E(x)=O_K(xe^{-c_K\sqrt{\log x}})\) for some \(c_K>0\).
For example, apply the unconditional effective Chebotarev theorem
to \(K/K\), use partial summation, and include the contributions
from \(j\geq2\). A possible fixed exceptional zero \(\beta<1\)
contributes \(O_K(x^\beta)\), which is absorbed in the same
estimate~\cite{LagariasOdlyzko1977}. In particular the two integrals
converge absolutely, and the prime-ideal counting function is
\(O_K(x/\log x)\).

Partial summation of \(\psi_K\) against \(1/(t\log t)\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-mertens}
 A(x)=\gamma+\log\kappa_K+\log\log x+
             \frac{E(x)}{x\log x}+H(x).
\end{equation}
To determine the constant, take logarithms in the third Mertens
theorem over \(K\)~\cite[Theorem 1]{GarciaLeeNumberFieldMertens}.
Its prime product, truncated by \(q\leq x\), differs in the
logarithm from \(A(x)\) by
\(\sum_{q\leq x}\sum_{j>k(q)}1/(jq^j)\), where
\(k(q)=\lfloor\log x/\log q\rfloor\). This difference is
\(O_K(x^{-1/2}/\log x)\). Indeed, the inner tail is
\(O(q^{-k(q)-1})\). For \(q\leq\sqrt{x}\) we bound it by \(O(1/x)\)
and count the prime ideals. For \(q>\sqrt{x}\) we bound it by
\(O(q^{-2})\), whose sum over those prime ideals has the asserted
order by partial summation. Thus
\(A(x)-\log\log x\longrightarrow\gamma+\log\kappa_K\).
Partial summation gives \eqref{eq:ideal-mertens} with this constant
because \(E(x)/(x\log x)\) and \(H(x)\) tend to zero.

If the exponent of \(\mathfrak p\) in \(\mathfrak a\) is \(k\geq1\),
then
\[
 \log\sum_{j=0}^k q^{-j}-\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{1}{jq^j}
 =\log(1-q^{-k-1})+\sum_{j>k}\frac{1}{jq^j}.
\]
The tail on the right is at most
\(q^{-k-1}/((k+1)(1-q^{-1}))\leq q^{-k-1}\).
The entire right side is nonpositive, and its absolute value is
\(O(q^{-k-1})\) with an absolute implied constant. When \(k=0\)
the discrepancy on the left is zero.

For \(x=\log N\mathfrak a>1\), the prime powers occurring in the
local factors of \(\sigma_K(\mathfrak a)/N\mathfrak a\) have total
cost \(\sum\log q=x\). Their term \(1/(jq^j)\) per unit cost is
\(1/(q^j\log q^j)\), decreasing with \(q^j\). Subtract
\((x\log x)^{-1}\) times the cost of each selected prime power.
The positive terms in the difference can occur only when \(q^j<x\).
Summing all such terms and adding back the total price yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-supporting-line}
 \log\frac{\sigma_K(\mathfrak a)}{N\mathfrak a}
 \leq A(x)+\frac{x-\psi_K(x)}{x\log x}
 =\gamma+\log\kappa_K+\log\log x+H(x).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The zero expansion and the explicit coefficient}

Write \((r_1,r_2)\) for the signature of \(K\). We use
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\) and
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\).
The function
\[
 \Xi_K(s)=s(s-1)|D_K|^{s/2}
       \Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)^{r_1}\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)^{r_2}
       \zeta_K(s)
\]
is entire of order at most one, satisfies \(\Xi_K(s)=\Xi_K(1-s)\),
and has precisely the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta_K\), counted with
multiplicity. The prime ideal estimate above bounds the integrated logarithmic
derivative as the line \(\Re s=1\) is approached from the right.
This excludes zeros on that line away from the pole, and the
functional equation gives the boundary at \(\Re s=0\). We use the standard completion
and its Hadamard product as in~\cite[\S\,3]{GrenieMolteniSmoothed}.

Set \(F_K(s)=(s-1)\zeta_K(s)\) and
\(\Phi(s)=(F_K'(s)/F_K(s)+1)/s\). Integration of the Dirichlet
series for \(-\zeta_K'/\zeta_K\) gives, for \(\Re s>1\),
\[
 F(s):=\int_1^\infty E(t)t^{-s-1}\,dt
   =-\frac{\zeta_K'(s)}{s\zeta_K(s)}-\frac{1}{s-1}
   =-\Phi(s).
\]
The prime ideal estimate gives \(F(1)=\int_1^\infty E(t)t^{-2}\,dt\)
and a removable value at \(s=1\). Fubini's theorem then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dedekind-h0-transform}
 \mathcal{M}(xH_0(x))(s)=\frac{F(s)-F(1)}{s-1}
                         =\frac{\Phi(s)-\Phi(1)}{1-s}.
\end{equation}

Here is the explicit inversion used for the coefficient. Write
\(\Xi_K'/\Xi_K(s)=b+\sum_{\rho}
((s-\rho)^{-1}+\rho^{-1})\). Let \(\psi_\Gamma=\Gamma'/\Gamma\).
The series
\(\psi_\Gamma(z)=-\gamma-z^{-1}
 +\sum_{n\geq1}z/(n(n+z))\) gives
\[
 \Phi(s)=\frac{B}{s}+\frac{r_1+r_2-1}{s^2}
  +\sum_\rho\frac{1}{\rho(s-\rho)}
  -r_1\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{1}{2n(s+2n)}
  -r_2\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{1}{n(s+n)}
\]
where \(B=b+1-\tfrac12\log|D_K|+\tfrac{r_1}{2}(\gamma+\log\pi)
+r_2(\gamma+\log(2\pi))\). The real-place terms lie on
the even negative lattice and the complex-place terms on the full
negative lattice. Taking the divided difference in
\eqref{eq:dedekind-h0-transform} gives the transform of
\begin{align}
 xH_0(x)={}& B+(r_1+r_2-1)(1+\log x)
       +\sum_\rho\frac{x^\rho}{\rho(1-\rho)}
 \nonumber\\
 &-r_1\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{x^{-2n}}{2n(1+2n)}
  -r_2\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{x^{-n}}{n(1+n)} .
 \label{eq:dedekind-h0-series}
\end{align}
For instance, \((s-\rho)^{-1}\) is the Mellin transform of \(x^\rho\).
The Hadamard product gives absolute summability of
\(|\rho(1-\rho)|^{-1}\), since the zero divisor has convergence
exponent at most one and has no point at \(0\) or \(1\). As
\(\Re\rho\leq1\), the zero series converges uniformly on compact
\(x\)-intervals and is absolutely integrable against \(x^{-3}\,dx\).
The analogous assertions for the two Gamma series follow from
their \(O(n^{-2})\) coefficients. Uniqueness on \(\Re s=2\)
and continuity give \eqref{eq:dedekind-h0-series} for \(x>1\).

Now assume Dedekind RH for \(K\). The zero series is bounded in
absolute value by \(B_K\sqrt{x}\), where
\[
 B_K=\sum_\rho \frac{1}{1/4+(\Im\rho)^2}.
\]
Subtract the values of \(\Xi_K'/\Xi_K\) at \(2\) and \(-1\).
The functional equation makes the difference
\(2\Xi_K'(2)/\Xi_K(2)\). A zero \(1/2+i\tau\) contributes
\(3/(9/4+\tau^2)\). Since
\((1/4+\tau^2)^{-1}\leq9/(9/4+\tau^2)\), we obtain
\[
 B_K\leq6\Re\frac{\Xi_K'(2)}{\Xi_K(2)}
       \leq9+3\log|D_K|.
\]
For the second inequality, \(\zeta_K'(2)/\zeta_K(2)\leq0\)
by the Euler product. The two archimedean log derivatives at \(2\)
are \((-\log\pi-\gamma)/2\) and
\(-\log(2\pi)+1-\gamma\), both negative. The terms left in the
upper estimate are \(1/2+1+\tfrac12\log|D_K|\).

In \eqref{eq:dedekind-h0-series} the nonzero-lattice Gamma series
are bounded and the remaining elementary terms are \(O_K(1+\log x)\).
Thus \(|H_0(x)|\leq (B_K+1)/\sqrt{x}\) for all sufficiently large
\(x\). Since \(H_0'(t)=E(t)/t^2\) almost everywhere, integration by
parts gives \(H(x)=w(x)H_0(x)+\int_x^\infty w'(t)H_0(t)\,dt\).
For \(t\geq e\), \(|w'(t)|\leq 3/(t\log^2 t)\). Therefore
\[
 H(x)\leq\frac{B_K+1}{\sqrt{x}\log x}
          +\frac{7(B_K+1)}{\sqrt{x}\log^2 x}
\]
eventually. In \eqref{eq:ideal-supporting-line}, exponentiation
multiplies \(\log x\) by \(e^{H(x)}\). The upper estimate on \(H\)
and \(e^u=1+u+O(u^2)\) for \(u\to0\) bound this by
\(\log x+(B_K+1+o_K(1))/\sqrt{x}\).
The strict inequality \(B_K+1\leq 10+3\log|D_K|<
12+3\log|D_K|\) yields \eqref{eq:ideal-robin}.

\subsection{The converse from least common multiples of ideals}

For \(x>1\), take
\[
 \mathfrak L_x=\prod_{\substack{\mathfrak p,\ j\geq1\\q^j\leq x}}\mathfrak p .
\]
Its logarithmic norm is \(\psi_K(x)\). The local discrepancy
computed above, summed with the same cutoff at \(\sqrt{x}\), gives
\[
 \log\frac{\sigma_K(\mathfrak L_x)}{N\mathfrak L_x}
        =A(x)+O_K\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\log x}\right).
\]
Since \(\psi_K(x)\sim x\), applying \eqref{eq:ideal-robin} and taking
logs gives the upper bound
\(\gamma+\log\kappa_K+\log\log\psi_K(x)
   +O_K(1/(\sqrt{x}\log x))\).
Concavity of \(\log\log t\) on \(t>1\) gives
\(\log\log\psi_K(x)\leq\log\log x+E(x)/(x\log x)\).
Comparison with \eqref{eq:ideal-mertens} then yields
\(H(x)\leq C_K/(\sqrt{x}\log x)\) eventually for some \(C_K\).

Let \(u=1/w\). For \(t>1\), \(0\leq u'(t)\leq1/t\).
Integrating \(H'(t)=w(t)E(t)/t^2\) by parts and using the prime ideal
estimate at infinity gives
\[
 H_0(x)=u(x)H(x)+\int_x^\infty H(t)u'(t)\,dt
       \leq C'_K x^{-1/2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(x\). This uses the upper bound on \(H\)
at every step, including the integral with its nonnegative weight.

By \eqref{eq:dedekind-h0-transform},
\(\mathcal{M}(xH_0(x))\) is meromorphic on \(\Re s>1/2\).
Its poles there occur exactly at zeros of \(\zeta_K\) in that
half-plane. The apparent singularity at \(1\) is removable.
At a real zero \(\beta\in(1/2,1)\) of multiplicity \(j\) the
transform has residue \(j/[\beta(1-\beta)]>0\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-sided-mellin} excludes all the poles, both real
and nonreal. Reflection under the functional equation gives Dedekind
RH. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-robin}.

\section{Dirichlet GRH and Dedekind ERH}\label{sec:dirichlet-erh}

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet GRH]\label{thm:dirichlet-cyclotomic}
Every zero in \(0<\Re s<1\) of every Dirichlet \(L(s,\chi)\), over
all positive moduli and all characters, has real part \(1/2\) if
and only if \eqref{eq:ideal-robin} holds in every cyclotomic field
\(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\), \(m\geq1\), with its own norm cutoff.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(G=(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\) and \(\chi\in\widehat G\),
let \(\chi^*\) denote the primitive character inducing \(\chi\).
The abelian zeta factorization in this case is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclotomic-product}
 \zeta_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)}(s)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat G}L(s,\chi^*).
\end{equation}
We check the Euler factors, including primes dividing \(m\).
For a rational prime \(\ell\), write \(m=\ell^a m'\) with
\((m',\ell)=1\). The inertia quotient is
\(G/I_\ell=(\mathbb Z/m'\mathbb Z)^\times\). The class of \(\ell\)
in this quotient is the Frobenius and has order \(f\), the residue
degree. There are \(g=|G/I_\ell|/f\) primes over \(\ell\),
each of norm \(\ell^f\). These assertions follow from cyclotomic
ramification and the Frobenius action on roots of unity
\cite[Ch.\,2 and 4]{WashingtonCyclotomic}.
The primitive character contributes a factor at \(\ell\) exactly
when \(\chi\) is trivial on \(I_\ell\). Among these characters every
\(f\)-th root of unity occurs \(g\) times at Frobenius, by restriction
of characters to its cyclic subgroup. The local product is thus
\((1-\ell^{-fs})^{-g}\), the Dedekind Euler factor. A trivial inertia
quotient has \(f=g=1\). Equality for \(\Re s>1\) gives
\eqref{eq:cyclotomic-product} by continuation.

All the factors in \eqref{eq:cyclotomic-product} are holomorphic
in the open strip. A zero of any one of them is a zero of the
product, and every zero of the product comes from a factor.
Every primitive character occurs in \eqref{eq:cyclotomic-product}
at its conductor. If \(\chi\) is imprimitive, then
\[
 L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi^*)\prod_{\ell\mid m,\;\ell\nmid
                  \operatorname{cond}(\chi^*)}
                    (1-\chi^*(\ell)\ell^{-s}).
\]
The extra factors have no zeros for \(\Re s>0\). Thus the open-strip
assertion for all the cyclotomic zeta functions is equivalent to
Dirichlet GRH. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-robin} in each cyclotomic
field.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dedekind ERH]\label{thm:dedekind-erh}
Every zero in \(0<\Re s<1\) of \(\zeta_K(s)\), for every number field
\(K\), has real part \(1/2\) if and only if \eqref{eq:ideal-robin} holds
in every number field.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-robin} for each \(K\). The threshold
in each inequality may depend on \(K\).
\end{proof}

In particular Dedekind ERH implies Dirichlet GRH. Over a general
base field \(K\), a finite-order Hecke character factors through
the Galois group of a finite abelian extension \(E/K\) by global
reciprocity~\cite{ArtinTateCFT}. The Artin factorization is
\[
  \zeta_E(s)=\prod_{\eta\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)}}L(s,\eta),
\]
where the factors are the Hecke \(L\)-functions with their primitive
local factors, and the trivial factor is \(\zeta_K(s)\).
All the factors are holomorphic in \(0<\Re s<1\). Thus a zero
off the critical line of any one factor would be a zero of
\(\zeta_E\) there. The trivial Hecke character over each \(K\)
gives the converse. Dedekind ERH is therefore equivalent to the
critical-line assertion over all fields for finite-order Hecke
characters. Brauer induction and induction invariance of Artin
\(L\)-functions write each Artin \(L\)-function as a quotient of
products of such Hecke factors over intermediate fields
\cite{BrauerArtin1947}. This also excludes Artin zeros and poles
off the critical line in the open critical strip under Dedekind ERH.
The next criterion includes Hecke characters of infinite order
and higher-rank standard functions.

\section{The triangular criterion for Grand RH}\label{sec:grand-research}

We use the unitary normalization of the standard \(L\)-function
\(L(s,\pi)\) of a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb A_K)\), with \(n\geq1\) and \(K\) any number
field. Its nontrivial zeros are the zeros of the pole-cleared
completed standard function. The completed functional equation is
centered at \(1/2\). Write \(\widetilde{\pi}\) for the contragredient.
A pure imaginary norm character \(|\cdot|_{\mathbb A_K}^{it}\) of
\(\mathrm{GL}_1\) has \(L(s,\pi)=\zeta_K(s+it)\); its zero condition is
the same as that of \(\zeta_K\) by vertical translation.

For each \(\pi\) other than those pure norm characters, take
\(Z(s)=L(s,\pi)L(s,\widetilde{\pi})\). Include the test \(Z=\zeta_K\)
for each \(K\) to cover all the pure norm characters over \(K\).
Let \(m\) be the pole order of \(Z\) at \(1\), so \(m=0\) for a
paired cuspidal product and \(m=1\) for \(\zeta_K\). For \(\Re s>1\),
write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard-log-derivative}
 -\frac{Z'}{Z}(s)=
   \sum_{\mathfrak p}\sum_{j\geq1}
      b_{\mathfrak p,j}(N\mathfrak p)^{-js}.
\end{equation}
Here the sum includes ramified local factors. If
\(L_{\mathfrak p}(s,\pi)=\prod_{k=1}^n
 (1-\alpha_{\mathfrak p,k}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}\),
allowing zero parameters at ramified primes, then
\(b_{\mathfrak p,j}=2\log N\mathfrak p\,
 \Re\bigl(\sum_k\alpha_{\mathfrak p,k}^j\bigr)\) for a pair.
For \(Z=\zeta_K\), \(b_{\mathfrak p,j}=\log N\mathfrak p\).
The pair is a product of two standard factors, whose individual
completed zeros are counted with their multiplicities.

\begin{theorem}[Grand RH]\label{thm:grand-triangular}
Every nontrivial zero of every standard \(L\)-function in this
unitary cuspidal family has real part \(1/2\) if and only if,
for every test \(Z\) defined above, there is a real \(C_Z\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grand-rh-prime-power}
 R_Z(x):=\frac{mx}{2}
  -\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak p,\ j\geq1\\(N\mathfrak p)^j\leq x}}
    b_{\mathfrak p,j}\left(1-\frac{(N\mathfrak p)^j}{x}\right)
   \leq C_Z\sqrt{x}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large \(x\). A term with \((N\mathfrak p)^j=x\)
has weight zero, so replacing \(\leq x\) by \(<x\) in the index set
gives the same sum. The constant and the cutoff may depend on \(Z\).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Analytic input for the standard family}

We use the standard continuation and functional equation of
Godement--Jacquet~\cite{GodementJacquet1972} and Tate in degree
one~\cite{Tate1967}. For the finite Euler products and their
absolute convergence on \(\Re s>1\), see
Jacquet--Shalika~\cite{JacquetShalika1981} and the summary over a
number field in~\cite[\S\,2.1]{ThornerZhaoRankinSelberg}. We use the
absolute convergence of the logarithmic series and of its derivative
on \(\Re s>1\). On the set \(I\) of unramified prime-power
indices \((\mathfrak p,j)\), write \(a_{\mathfrak p,j}\) for the
standard power sum \(\sum_k\alpha_{\mathfrak p,k}^j\).
The corresponding self Rankin--Selberg coefficients are
\(|a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2\), so at every real \(\sigma>1\),
\[
 \sum_I\frac{|a_{\mathfrak p,j}|}{j q^{j\sigma}}
 \leq
 \left(\sum_I\frac{|a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2}{j q^{j\sigma}}\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_I\frac{1}{j q^{j\sigma}}\right)^{1/2}<\infty,
 \qquad q=N\mathfrak p.
\]
The two factors are finite by the respective log Euler products.
The extra logarithmic factor from differentiation is absorbed by
taking absolute convergence at a slightly smaller real part
still above \(1\). There are finitely many ramified primes, and
their local log series converge absolutely there by
\cite[(2.2)]{ThornerZhaoRankinSelberg}. This also gives
nonvanishing on \(\Re s>1\).

In the same notation, the archimedean factors are products of
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+\mu)\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s+\mu)\),
with \(\Re\mu>-1/2\) by~\cite[(2.2)]{ThornerZhaoRankinSelberg}.
The conductor factor is \(Q^{s/2}\) with \(Q>0\).
The pole-cleared completion has order at most one and satisfies
the functional equation with a nonzero root number,
as recorded in~\cite[\S\,2.1]{ThornerZhaoRankinSelberg}.
For a pair, multiply the completions of the two individual factors.
For the separate test \(Z=\zeta_K\), use \(\Xi_K\). This gives a
pole-cleared completed function
\[
 s^m Q_Z^{s/2}G_Z(s)F_Z(s),\qquad F_Z(s)=(s-1)^m Z(s),
\]
where \(G_Z\) is a product of the indicated Gamma factors.
The cleared ordinary function \(F_Z\) is entire of order at most
one. Indeed, the completed function and the reciprocal Gamma and
conductor factors have order at most one. Division by \(s^m\)
has this same growth bound outside the unit disk, and the
singularity at \(0\) is removable for \(F_Z\).
On \(\Re s>1/2\), every completion factor in this display is
finite and nonzero. The functional equation sends a completed
zero at \(s\) to a completed zero of the dual at \(1-s\). Thus
Grand RH is equivalent to nonvanishing of each \(F_Z\) on
\(\Re s>1/2\). In the paired case, a zero of either factor
gives a zero of their product, since both factors are entire.

\subsection{Mellin transform of the prime-power remainder}

For \(v>1\) and \(\Re s>1\),
\[
 \int_v^\infty (1-v/x)x^{-s-1}\,dx=\frac{v^{-s}}{s(s+1)}.
\]
The absolute convergence of \eqref{eq:standard-log-derivative}
justifies summing these integrals. Using
\(F_Z'/F_Z=m/(s-1)+Z'/Z\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grand-mellin-transform}
 \mathcal{M}R_Z(s)
 =\frac{m}{2(s-1)}+\frac{Z'(s)}{s(s+1)Z(s)}
 =\frac{F_Z'(s)/F_Z(s)+m(s+2)/2}{s(s+1)}.
\end{equation}
The pole correction on the prime side cancels the contribution
of the pole of \(Z\) at \(1\).

If \eqref{eq:grand-rh-prime-power} holds, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-sided-mellin} with \(\alpha=1/2\).
On \(\Re s>1/2\) the poles of \eqref{eq:grand-mellin-transform}
are exactly the zeros of \(F_Z\). At a real zero \(\beta>1/2\)
of multiplicity \(j\) the residue is \(j/(\beta(\beta+1))>0\).
The lemma excludes every pole in this half-plane. Completion
and reflection yield Grand RH.

Conversely, suppose \(F_Z\) is nonvanishing on \(\Re s>1/2\).
In its genus-one logarithmic derivative expansion let \(b\) be
the constant term after isolating the zeros at \(0\) and \(-1\).
Write \(m_0,m_{-1}\) for their multiplicities. With zeros counted
with multiplicity, the expansion is
\[
 \frac{F_Z'}{F_Z}(s)
   = b+\frac{m_0}{s}+\frac{m_{-1}}{s+1}
     +\sum_{\rho\notin\{0,-1\}}
                  \left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\right).
\]
Each term \((s-\rho)^{-1}+\rho^{-1}\), divided by \(s(s+1)\),
has inverse Mellin transform
\((x^\rho-x^{-1})/(\rho(\rho+1))\).
The transforms of \(1-x^{-1}\), \(1-\tfrac12x^{-1}\), and
\(\log x-1+x^{-1}\) supply the first three elementary terms in
the inversion of \eqref{eq:grand-mellin-transform}. For the zero
at \(-1\), the partial fractions are
\[
 \frac{1}{s(s+1)^2}=\frac1s-\frac1{s+1}-\frac1{(s+1)^2}.
\]
Hence
\begin{align}
 R_Z(x)=\Re\Bigg[&
 b(1-x^{-1})+m(1-\tfrac12x^{-1})
 +m_0(\log x-1+x^{-1})
 +m_{-1}(1-x^{-1}-x^{-1}\log x)
 \nonumber\\
 &+\sum_{\rho\notin\{0,-1\}}
             \frac{x^\rho-x^{-1}}{\rho(\rho+1)}\Bigg].
 \label{eq:grand-zeros-inversion}
\end{align}
The zero weights \(1/|\rho(\rho+1)|\) are summable, since the
zero divisor of \(F_Z\) has convergence exponent at most one.
Every \(\Re\rho\leq 1/2\). The zero series is locally uniformly
convergent and dominated by a constant times \(\sqrt{x}+x^{-1}\).
These bounds justify termwise transformation at \(\Re s=2\) and
give pointwise equality by uniqueness and continuity. The prime
side is continuous even at a prime-power threshold, since the
weight there is zero. The elementary terms in
\eqref{eq:grand-zeros-inversion} are \(O_Z(1+\log x)\).
It follows that \(|R_Z(x)|=O_Z(\sqrt{x})\), proving
\eqref{eq:grand-rh-prime-power} and the theorem.

\subsection{Specializations}

For \(Z=\zeta_K\), integrating the step function \(\psi_K\) gives
\[
 R_{\zeta_K}(x)=\frac{x}{2}-\frac{1}{x}\int_0^x\psi_K(t)\,dt .
\]
By the fieldwise arguments in Sections~\ref{sec:grh}
and~\ref{sec:grand-research}, this triangular bound is
equivalent to \eqref{eq:ideal-robin} for that field. Restricting
these equivalent tests to the cyclotomic fields yields
Theorem~\ref{thm:dirichlet-cyclotomic}. For a
unitary Hecke character \(\chi\) outside the pure norm family,
the paired coefficient is
\[
 b_{\mathfrak p,j}=2\Re(\alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)^j)\log N\mathfrak p,
 \qquad \alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)=0\text{ at ramified primes}.
\]
This is the pole-free degree-one case of
\eqref{eq:grand-rh-prime-power}. The remaining unitary Hecke
characters are the norm twists \(\chi_t=|\cdot|^{it}\). Their
pole-cleared completions are translates of the Dedekind
completion by \(s\mapsto s+it\). It follows that for a fixed
\(K\) the critical-line condition for the entire rank-one family
is equivalent to the Dedekind triangular bound together with
the paired triangular bound for each unitary Hecke character
nontrivial on norm-one idele classes. This specialization is
verified in Appendix~\ref{app:hecke-formalization}.
Products of unitary cuspidal standard factors inherit the
completed zero condition from their factors.

\subsection{Degree-one primes and a nonabelian example}

Write \(a_K(p)\) for the number of prime ideals of \(K\) above a
rational prime \(p\) with residue degree one. Put
\[
 \Theta_K^{(1)}(x)=\sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
     a_K(p)\log p\,(1-p/x).
\]
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degree-one-primes}
For a fixed number field \(K\), the corrected ideal Robin criterion
\eqref{eq:ideal-robin} is equivalent to the existence of a constant
\(C_K\) such that
\[
 \Theta_K^{(1)}(x)\ \geq\ x/2-C_K\sqrt{x}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(x\). Consequently Dedekind ERH is
equivalent to this lower bound in every number field.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(f_{\mathfrak p}\) and
\(e_{\mathfrak p}\) be the residue degree and ramification index
of \(\mathfrak p\) over \(p\). At any given power \(p^k\) the
coefficient of the prime-ideal sum for \(Z=\zeta_K\) is
\[
 \log p\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak p\mid p\\ f_{\mathfrak p}\mid k}}
       f_{\mathfrak p} \ \leq\ d\log p
\]
by \(\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p}e_{\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak p}=d\).
The coefficient at \(k=1\) is \(a_K(p)\log p\).
Take an absolute Chebyshev constant \(C_\vartheta\) such that
\(\sum_{p\le y,\;p\ \mathrm{prime}}\log p\le C_\vartheta y\) for
\(y\ge2\). The total contribution \(E_K(x)\) of \(k\ge2\) in the
triangular sum satisfies, for \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\leq E_K(x)
 \leq d C_\vartheta\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor} x^{1/k}
 \leq d C_\vartheta\left(1+\frac{6}{e\log 2}\right)\sqrt{x}.
\]
For the last inequality the terms with \(k\ge3\) are bounded by
\((\log_2 x)x^{1/3}\), and \(u e^{-u/6}\le6/e\) for \(u\ge0\).
Since \(R_{\zeta_K}(x)=x/2-\Theta_K^{(1)}(x)-E_K(x)\), the two
one-sided bounds are equivalent after a change in their constants.
The specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:grand-triangular} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-robin} complete the proof.
\end{proof}

It is enough to verify the degree-one criterion in finite Galois
extensions \(L/\mathbb Q\). For the normal closure \(L\) of any
\(K\), the Aramata--Brauer theorem makes \(\zeta_L/\zeta_K\)
entire, so every open-strip zero of \(\zeta_K\) is a zero of
\(\zeta_L\)~\cite{GunHazraSahuArtin}. For a Galois \(L/\mathbb Q\)
an unramified prime has degree-one factors precisely when it
splits completely, in which case it has \([L:\mathbb Q]\) such
factors. The finitely many ramified primes contribute \(O_L(1)\).
Consequently Dedekind ERH is also equivalent to
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\ \mathrm{prime\ splitting\ completely\ in}\ L}}
       \log p\,(1-p/x)
 \ \geq\ \frac{x}{2[L:\mathbb Q]}-C_L\sqrt{x}
\]
eventually for every finite Galois \(L/\mathbb Q\), with a constant
and a threshold depending on \(L\).

For \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})\), the Dedekind--Kummer theorem
identifies \(a_K(p)\) for \(p\nmid 6\) with the number of roots of
\(T^3-2\) in \(\mathbb F_p\)~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{SutherlandArithmeticEquivalence}.
The cubic map on \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) is bijective for
\(p\equiv2\bmod 3\). For \(p\equiv1\bmod 3\), the root count is
three if \(2^{(p-1)/3}\equiv1\bmod p\), and zero otherwise.
Under rational RH,
\(\Theta_{\mathbb Q}^{(1)}(x)=x/2+O(\sqrt{x})\) by the
two-sided estimate from \eqref{eq:grand-zeros-inversion} and the
same bound for \(E_{\mathbb Q}\).
The finitely many excluded primes contribute \(O(1)\).
Thus RH for this cubic field is equivalent, given rational RH,
to the bound
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\le x,\ p\ \mathrm{prime}\\ p\equiv1\bmod 3}}
  \left(3\mathbf 1_{\{2^{(p-1)/3}\equiv1\bmod p\}}-1\right)
  \log p\,(1-p/x) \ \geq\ -C\sqrt{x}
\]
with some \(C\) for all sufficiently large \(x\). This gives
an explicit nonabelian prime-splitting sum on which to test an
extension of the Dirichlet method.

For a paired factor \(Z=L(s,\pi)L(s,\widetilde\pi)\), there is a
related exact comparison of nonnegative sums. Let
\(I_\pi(x)=\{(\mathfrak p,j):\mathfrak p\text{ unramified for }\pi,\,
j\ge1,\ q^j\le x\}\), where \(q=N\mathfrak p\), and put
\(a_{\mathfrak p,j}=\sum_k\alpha_{\mathfrak p,k}^j\).
The identity
\[
 2\Re a_{\mathfrak p,j}
  = |1+a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2-|a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2-1
\]
gives the equivalent bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grand-rankin-comparison}
 \sum_{I_\pi(x)} |1+a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2
                \log q\,(1-q^j/x)
 \ \geq\
 \sum_{I_\pi(x)} (|a_{\mathfrak p,j}|^2+1)
                \log q\,(1-q^j/x)-C_\pi\sqrt{x}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, choose \(\theta_n<\eta<1/2\) with \(\theta_n\) from
\cite[(2.2)]{ThornerZhaoRankinSelberg}. Each omitted finite prime
contributes at most \(2n(\log q)x^\eta/(1-q^{-\eta})\) in
absolute value, by a geometric sum over its powers. The
omitted contribution is therefore \(O_\pi(\sqrt{x})\).
The two squares in \eqref{eq:grand-rankin-comparison}
are the unramified self Rankin--Selberg power sums for
\(1\boxplus\pi\) and \(\pi\). The estimate concerns their difference
after including the trivial factor. Positivity of the individual
sums alone gives no bound of this size.

If a paired standard factor has \(|\alpha_{\mathfrak p,k}|\le1\)
at all finite places, its coefficients satisfy
\(|b_{\mathfrak p,j}|\le2n\log N\mathfrak p\).
The same calculation then bounds the absolute contribution of
the terms of rational norm \(p^k\), \(k\ge2\), by
\(2nd C_\vartheta(1+6/(e\log 2))\sqrt{x}\).
For that factor the degree-one prime terms also suffice.
The general triangular criterion retains all prime powers and
does not require these bounds on the local parameters.

In either setting one can first seek a lower bound with error
\(C_\varepsilon x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
holding eventually with the constant and threshold allowed to
depend on the fixed field or factor. Lemma~\ref{lem:one-sided-mellin}
applied with \(\alpha=1/2+\varepsilon\) excludes any pole to the
right of that line. Taking all \(\varepsilon>0\) gives the
square-root criterion by the already proved reverse implication.

\section{Critical-line location with prescribed multiplicities}
\label{sec:multiplicity}

The triangular bound controls the locations of the zeros. A second test
records the order at each point, including zeros shared by several
factors. We use the sublevel-area test for simple zeros developed in
\emph{Prime Factor Oscillations} and extend its threshold to any
nonnegative integer.\footnote{See
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations/blob/5aa03e03423b7f9cee75f31798e9d68dde8c5dbf/PrimeFactorOscillations/Helpers/ZeroSmallValueArea.lean}{\texttt{ZeroSmallValueArea.lean}}
and the coprimality application
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations/blob/5aa03e03423b7f9cee75f31798e9d68dde8c5dbf/PrimeFactorOscillations/Assembly/CarmichaelSimplicityCriterion.lean}{\texttt{CarmichaelSimplicityCriterion.lean}}.}
For a holomorphic function \(F\) with a nonzero germ at \(w\), let
\[
 A_F(w,R,\eta)=
 \operatorname{area}\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-w|<R,\ |F(z)|\leq\eta\}.
\]
For every integer \(B\geq 0\),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{ord}_w F\leq B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists R>0:\quad
 A_F(w,R,\varepsilon^{B+1})=o(\varepsilon^2)
 \quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
 \label{eq:order-area-threshold}
\end{equation}
The disk on the right is taken inside the domain of \(F\).
Indeed, if \(m=\operatorname{ord}_w F>0\), write
\(F(z)=(z-w)^m g(z)\) with \(g(w)\ne0\). On a sufficiently small disk,
\(|g|\) lies between two positive constants. The small-value set lies
between disks of radii proportional to \(\eta^{1/m}\) for small enough
\(\eta\), so its area is bounded above and below by positive multiples of
\(\eta^{2/m}\). The lower bound works in every disk about \(w\) by first
restricting to a smaller disk. The exponent \(2(B+1)/m\) exceeds \(2\)
exactly when \(m\le B\). For \(m=0\) the area vanishes for all sufficiently
small thresholds in some disk. In particular, \(B=1\) is the original
test \(A_F(w,R,\eta)=o(\eta)\).

Suppose a chosen factorization on the strip has the form
\[
 F(s)=U(s)\prod_{j=1}^{r}F_j(s)^{e_j},
\]
where \(U\) is holomorphic and nowhere zero, the \(F_j\) are holomorphic
with nonzero germs, and \(e_j\) are positive integers. Fix lower bounds
\(b_j(w)\le\operatorname{ord}_w F_j\). The basic choice is \(b_j(w)=1\)
at a zero of \(F_j\) and \(0\) elsewhere. A known order or a larger proved
lower bound can be used at an exceptional point. Set
\(B_F(w)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}e_j b_j(w)\). Additivity of zero order gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_w F\ge B_F(w)\); equality means each factor attains
its specified lower bound. At a common zero the contributions add.
If simplicity is required away from a factor's intrinsic center, its
condition at that center can instead be omitted in the factorwise test.
If an exact central order is prescribed, it can be tested by applying
\eqref{eq:order-area-threshold} at that order and, for positive order,
requiring failure at the preceding integer.

Thus the combined location and multiplicity assertion is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:order-area-threshold} at every point of the open critical strip
with cap \(0\) off the line and cap \(B_F(w)\) on the line. Equivalently,
one can use the corrected Robin inequality or the triangular inequality
from the corresponding theorem for location, and test area only on the
line. Multiplying by a holomorphic unit preserves the order and hence
either area test. We may therefore use the following arithmetic
carriers, whose identities hold first in the half-plane of absolute
convergence and then by continuation:
\begin{align*}
 C_{\chi}(s)
 &=\frac{L(s,\chi)}{L(s+1,\chi)}
   =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\chi(n)\varphi(n)}{n^{s+1}},\\
 C_K(s)
 &=\frac{\zeta_K(s)}{\zeta_K(s+1)}
   =\sum_{\mathfrak a\ne(0)}
     \frac{\varphi_K(\mathfrak a)}{(N\mathfrak a)^{s+1}},
 \qquad
 \varphi_K(\mathfrak a)=N\mathfrak a
         \prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak a}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-1}).
\end{align*}
The ideal sum is over nonzero integral ideals. Both identities follow
by taking local geometric series with the coprimality weight at each
positive exponent. The denominators are nonzero on the strip. For an
individual automorphic \(L\)-function use
\(C_\pi(s)=Z_\pi(s)/L(s+1,\pi)\), where \(Z_\pi\) is its individual
pole-cleared completion. The finite part \(L(s,\pi)/L(s+1,\pi)\) has local
factors
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^n
   \frac{1-\alpha_{\pi,j}(\mathfrak p)q^{-s-1}}
        {1-\alpha_{\pi,j}(\mathfrak p)q^{-s}},
 \qquad q=N\mathfrak p .
\]
For each Satake coordinate the factor at exponent \(k\ge1\) is
\((1-q^{-1})\alpha_{\pi,j}(\mathfrak p)^k\), with constant term \(1\).
Use the ramified parameters from the local standard factor, padded by
zeros as above. The individual completions measure the orders of the
original functions. Testing a paired completion instead adds the orders
of both members.

For Dirichlet GRH the natural additional requirement is simplicity
for each character. Finite Euler factors for imprimitive characters
are units in the open strip. For Dedekind ERH use the exponents of the
specified holomorphic factorization, for example the character
factorization of an abelian field. In a full irreducible Artin
factorization, the exponents are the multiplicities in the permutation
representation. Using all these factors as holomorphic functions for
an arbitrary field requires their holomorphy. Even in the known
holomorphic cases, the exponents can exceed one. For the \(S_3\)
splitting field of \(X^3-2\), the standard two-dimensional constituent
occurs twice in the regular representation. A zero of its holomorphic
\(L\)-function therefore contributes at least two to the order of the
Dedekind zeta function. For Grand RH, apply the criterion to each
individual cuspidal standard completion and specify any intrinsic
central exceptions. Higher central orders can occur already in degree
two; the level \(389\) elliptic curve \texttt{389a1} supplies an
example.\footnote{\href{https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/389a1/}
{LMFDB entry for \texttt{389a1}} records analytic rank two.}
The location equivalences above and the area threshold together give
criteria with the chosen multiplicity requirements. Proving the
resulting area bounds is an additional assertion.

\subsection{Multiplicity from the same prime-power remainder}

For a triangular remainder \(R_Z\) as in
\eqref{eq:grand-mellin-transform}, its square-root bound is equivalent
to the location condition, and the forward direction of that equivalence
gives \(|R_Z(x)|=O_Z(\sqrt{x})\). Under these equivalent conditions,
\(\mathcal{M}R_Z(s)\) is an absolutely convergent integral for
\(\Re s>1/2\). Equation~\eqref{eq:grand-mellin-transform} holds there by
analytic continuation. Let \(w=1/2+i\gamma\), \(s=w+\delta\) with
\(\delta>0\), and set
\[
 D_Z(w,\delta)=\delta\,s(s+1)
       \int_1^\infty R_Z(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx .
\]
The logarithmic derivative of \(F_Z\) is
\(\operatorname{ord}_w F_Z/(s-w)\) plus a function holomorphic in a
neighborhood of \(w\). Thus
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}D_Z(w,\delta)=\operatorname{ord}_w F_Z.
\]
The limit exists also at a nonzero value of \(F_Z\), where it is zero.
Consequently the area bound at a prescribed maximum order
\(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) has a test involving the very same arithmetic
remainder:
\begin{equation}
 \Re D_Z(w,\delta)\ \le\ B+\tfrac12
 \qquad\text{for every sufficiently small }\delta>0 .
 \label{eq:triangular-order-test}
\end{equation}
The half-integer threshold uses the fact that the limit is an integer.
Changing variables \(x=e^u\) identifies the integral as the exponentially
damped Fourier transform of \(e^{-u/2}R_Z(e^u)\) at frequency \(\gamma\).
Thus the square-root bound and the boundary tests
\eqref{eq:triangular-order-test} use one arithmetic input. The order of
the full completion agrees with this limit when the additional completion
factor is a unit at \(w\). In general, if that factor is \(G_Z\), the
expression for the full completion includes
\(\delta G_Z'(s)/G_Z(s)\). For a holomorphic product, the boundary
test with the sum of proved lower bounds as cap enforces equality
factor by factor because each excess order is nonnegative.

\subsection{A global mean-square criterion}

One can test all ordinates together. Assume the location inequality for
\(R_Z\) and put \(A_Z(t)=e^{-t/2}R_Z(e^t)\) for \(t\ge0\), extended by
zero for \(t<0\). Write \(\rho_\gamma=1/2+i\gamma\) and
\(m_\gamma=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_\gamma}F_Z\). In the following sums,
the ordinates with \(m_\gamma>0\) are distinct. The inversion
\eqref{eq:grand-zeros-inversion} gives
\begin{equation}
 A_Z(t)=
 \sum_{\gamma}
 \frac{m_\gamma}{\rho_\gamma(\rho_\gamma+1)}\,e^{i\gamma t}
 +o(1)\qquad(t\to\infty).
 \label{eq:multiplicity-log-series}
\end{equation}
The sum converges absolutely and uniformly. Zeros strictly to the
left of the line contribute \(o(1)\) by dominated convergence using
the summable inverse Mellin weights. For the real paired data, the
conjugate zeros make the displayed series real. For an individual
complex factor, use its own complex prime-power coefficients and
omit the real part in the inversion, which follows from the same
absolute convergence and uniqueness. Pure norm translates use
the corresponding translated ordinate and polar term.

Use the Fourier convention
\(\widehat k(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb R}k(v)e^{-i\gamma v}\,dv\).
For \(a=1,2\), put
\[
 g_a(v)=\frac{e^{-v^2/(4a)}}{\sqrt{4\pi a}},\qquad
 k_a=\Bigl(\frac{d}{dv}+\tfrac12\Bigr)
     \Bigl(\frac{d}{dv}+\tfrac32\Bigr)g_a,\qquad
 H_a(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}k_a(v)A_Z(t-v)\,dv .
\]
In particular, \(\widehat k_a(\gamma)=
\rho_\gamma(\rho_\gamma+1)e^{-a\gamma^2}\).
Let \(E\subset\mathbb R\) be a finite set of ordinates at which a
different order is allowed. Define \(\widetilde H_a(t)\) by subtracting,
for \(\gamma\in E\), the terms \(h_a(\gamma)e^{i\gamma t}\), where
\[
 h_a(\gamma)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T
               \int_0^T H_a(t)e^{-i\gamma t}\,dt .
\]
The basic case is \(E=\varnothing\). All transforms
use the arithmetic remainder and the chosen exceptional ordinates.

\begin{equation}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T|\widetilde H_1(t)|^2\,dt
 \ \le\ \limsup_{t\to\infty}\Re\widetilde H_2(t)
 \label{eq:global-multiplicity-energy}
\end{equation}
holds if and only if every zero on the line outside \(E\) is simple.
To see this, convolve \eqref{eq:multiplicity-log-series} termwise
with \(k_a\in L^1(\mathbb R)\). The remainder is bounded and tends
to zero at positive infinity, so its convolution also tends to zero.
The smoothed series has coefficient
\(m_\gamma e^{-a\gamma^2}\). Averaging its product with
\(e^{-i\gamma t}\) recovers that coefficient, giving the asserted
exception removal.\footnote{For logarithmic frequency recovery in the
zeta setting, see
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations/blob/5aa03e03423b7f9cee75f31798e9d68dde8c5dbf/PrimeFactorOscillations/Helpers/XiZeroLogSpectrum.lean}{\texttt{XiZeroLogSpectrum.lean}}.}
Orthogonality of distinct frequencies in the mean and absolute
convergence give the left side of
\eqref{eq:global-multiplicity-energy} as
\(\sum_{\gamma\notin E}m_\gamma^2e^{-2\gamma^2}\).
The real part of the \(a=2\) series is at most the sum of its
nonnegative coefficients. It approaches that bound arbitrarily far
out. For a finite set of frequencies, take the difference of two
arbitrarily distant indices in a convergent subsequence of
\((e^{i\gamma n})_\gamma\) in the finite torus. At those differences all
the phases approach \(1\). Absolute convergence makes the tail of the
infinite series arbitrarily small. The right side is therefore
\(\sum_{\gamma\notin E}m_\gamma e^{-2\gamma^2}\).
Their difference is the sum of
\(m_\gamma(m_\gamma-1)e^{-2\gamma^2}\), which vanishes exactly
when every retained zero is simple.

For a chosen positive factorization, apply
\eqref{eq:global-multiplicity-energy} to the individual factors and
sum the two sides with the factorization weights \(e_j\). This gives
one inequality for the product. A common zero contributes once to
each factor's test, and its order in the product is the weighted
sum. To test a prescribed exact order \(r\) at an exceptional
ordinate \(\gamma\), add
\(\lvert h_1(\gamma)-r e^{-\gamma^2}\rvert^2\) to the left side for
that factor. Under the location criterion, the resulting inequality
requires those exact orders and simplicity at every remaining
zero. If \(G_Z\) is the additional factor in the full completion,
set \(g_\gamma=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_\gamma}G_Z\), allowing negative
orders at poles. Before removing exceptional frequencies, add
\[
 \sum_{\gamma:\,g_\gamma\ne0}
       g_\gamma e^{-a\gamma^2}e^{i\gamma t}
\]
to \(H_a\). The sum is finite for the gamma, exponential, and
polynomial factors used here. The resulting coefficients use the
orders in the full completion. When \(G_Z\) is a unit on the line,
the inversion in \eqref{eq:multiplicity-log-series} gives these
multiplicities directly.

\subsection{Four conditional multiplicity equivalences}

Write \(\mathcal E(Z;E)\) for the mean-square limit on the left of
\eqref{eq:global-multiplicity-energy}, including the completion
correction where needed. Write \(\mathcal S(Z;E)\) for the limit superior
on the right. For a finite subset \(J\subseteq E\) and prescribed
orders \(r(\gamma)\ge0\) on \(J\), put
\[
 \mathcal P(Z;J,r)
 =\sum_{\gamma\in J}
       \bigl|h_1(\gamma)-r(\gamma)e^{-\gamma^2}\bigr|^2.
\]
Here \(h_1\) is computed before removing the ordinates in \(E\).
Thus \(E\setminus J\) permits arbitrary orders, and \(J\) prescribes
exact orders.

\begin{theorem}[RH and simplicity]\label{thm:rh-multiplicity}
Assume the Riemann hypothesis. Every nontrivial zero of \(\zeta\)
is simple if and only if
\[
 \mathcal E(\zeta;\varnothing)\le\mathcal S(\zeta;\varnothing).
\]
The input is \(R_Z\) for \(Z=\zeta_{\mathbb Q}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet GRH and simplicity]\label{thm:grh-multiplicity}
Assume Dirichlet GRH. Every nontrivial zero of every Dirichlet
\(L\)-function is simple if and only if, for every Dirichlet character
\(\chi\),
\[
 \mathcal E(L(\cdot,\chi);\varnothing)
 \le\mathcal S(L(\cdot,\chi);\varnothing).
\]
It suffices on both sides to take primitive characters.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Dedekind ERH and multiplicity in a factorization]
\label{thm:erh-multiplicity}
Assume Dedekind ERH. Fix a positive holomorphic factorization
\(\zeta_K=U_K\prod_{j\in I_K}L_{K,j}^{e_{K,j}}\) on the strip for
each field in the test, with \(U_K\) a unit and factors admitting
the prime-power inversion above. Then for every such \(K\) and
\(w\) on the line,
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_w\zeta_K
  =\sum_{j\in I_K}e_{K,j}\,\mathbf1_{\{L_{K,j}(w)=0\}}
\]
if and only if, for every such \(K\),
\[
 \sum_{j\in I_K}e_{K,j}\mathcal E(L_{K,j};\varnothing)
 \le
 \sum_{j\in I_K}e_{K,j}\mathcal S(L_{K,j};\varnothing).
\]
In a full holomorphic Artin factorization the weights are the
irreducible multiplicities of the permutation representation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Grand RH and multiplicity of standard \(L\)-functions]
\label{thm:grand-multiplicity}
Assume Grand RH. For each individual unitary cuspidal standard
\(L\)-function over a number field, fix \(J_\pi\subseteq E_\pi\)
and \(r_\pi\) as above. All nontrivial zeros outside \(E_\pi\)
are simple and all orders on \(J_\pi\) equal \(r_\pi\), for every
\(\pi\), if and only if, for every \(\pi\),
\[
 \mathcal E(L(\cdot,\pi);E_\pi)
 +\mathcal P(L(\cdot,\pi);J_\pi,r_\pi)
 \le \mathcal S(L(\cdot,\pi);E_\pi).
\]
An intrinsic center can be included in \(E_\pi\) with its order
left free or placed in \(J_\pi\) with an exact order specified.
For a pure norm twist of the trivial character use the untranslated
zeta function, shifting the prescribed ordinates accordingly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The RH and Dirichlet statements apply
\eqref{eq:global-multiplicity-energy} to the individual functions.
The additional Euler factors of an imprimitive character are units
in the open strip. Positivity of the holomorphic factorization in
the Dedekind statement means that every zero of a factor in the
open strip is a zero of \(\zeta_K\), hence is on the line under ERH.
The difference of the two weighted sums is nonnegative and is zero
precisely when each factor has only simple zeros. Additivity of
orders gives the displayed equality, including coincidences between
different factors. For Grand RH the difference of the two sides is
again a sum of nonnegative multiplicity terms plus the squares in
\(\mathcal P\). Each vanishes precisely at the claimed orders.
\end{proof}

The four equivalences assume the corresponding location hypothesis.
Combining any of them with the location equivalence gives a
simultaneous arithmetic criterion for location and the specified
orders. The holomorphic factorization in the Dedekind statement is
part of its data. An application using all irreducible Artin factors
of an arbitrary field requires the holomorphy of those factors.

\section{The classical Robin criteria}\label{app:classical-criteria}

For a positive integer $n$, let
\[
  \sig(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d,
  \qquad
  \A(n)=\frac{\sig(n)}{n},
\]
and let $\euler$ be the Euler--Mascheroni constant.  Robin's theorem is the
following exact criterion.  Throughout, $\RH$ denotes the standard
zero-locus formulation: every zero $s$ of the analytically continued
Riemann zeta function which is neither a negative even integer nor the pole
at $1$ satisfies $\Re s=1/2$.  This is also Mathlib's definition of
\leanname{RiemannHypothesis}; it is stronger in presentation, but equivalent
in the usual way, to saying that zeta has no zero with $\Re s>1/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Robin~\cite{Robin1984}]\label{thm:main}
The Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:robin}
  \sig(n)<e^{\euler}n\log\log n
\end{equation}
for every integer $n>5040$.
\end{theorem}

The cutoff is part of the theorem.  In particular, the result is not merely
an eventual estimate.  It combines an analytic argument above an explicit
height with a finite proof below that height.

Robin's original article~\cite{Robin1984} is in French.  Although a scanned
PDF is now available online, the paper remains comparatively difficult to
discover through ordinary bibliographic channels.  One purpose of the present
paper is therefore expository: to give an accessible English, proof-oriented
account of the argument while preserving the attribution and source trail of
the original.  Nicolas's related Euler-function criterion has a later English
treatment~\cite{Nicolas2012}; the present paper concerns Robin's distinct
sum-of-divisors argument.

The complete Lean source, certificate data, reproducibility instructions,
and release artifacts are available at

  \url{\repositoryurl}.
\end{center}
Section~\ref{sec:formalization} links the public theorem boundary and the
principal modules for each mathematical stage, while the finite-range
section links the exact certificate rows directly.

The second principal result makes Robin's extremal reduction explicit.  For
$\eps>0$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ca-objective}
  Q_{\eps}(m)=\frac{\sig(m)}{m^{1+\eps}}
             =\frac{\A(m)}{m^{\eps}}.
\end{equation}
We say that $N>1$ is colossally abundant with parameter $\eps$ if
$Q_{\eps}(m)\le Q_{\eps}(N)$ for every $m>1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ca-main}
The Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if \eqref{eq:robin} holds for every
$N>5040$ which is colossally abundant with some positive parameter.
\end{theorem}

Colossally abundant numbers were introduced by Alaoglu and
Erd\H{o}s~\cite{AlaogluErdos1944}.  The criterion itself and its use of those

Nicolas~\cite{Nicolas1983} together with Landau's positive-transform
principle~\cite{Landau1905}.  The explicit estimates for prime functions used
below trace to Rosser and Schoenfeld~\cite{RosserSchoenfeld1962} and to Costa
Pereira~\cite{CostaPereira1985,CostaPereira1987}.  Our purpose is not to claim
new priority for those ingredients, but to give a complete proof whose exact

\subsection{What Robin's inequality measures}

The ratio $\A(n)=\sig(n)/n$ measures how rich $n$ is in divisors relative to
its size.  If
\[
 n=\prod_p p^{a_p},
\]
then $\A(n)$ is a product of local factors, one for each prime dividing $n$.
Small primes increase this ratio most efficiently, and raising a prime to a
higher power gives progressively smaller additional gains.  Integers built
from many small prime powers are therefore the difficult cases for an upper
bound on $\A(n)$.

Robin's inequality says that even these difficult integers satisfy
\[
 \A(n)<e^\euler\log\log n
\]
once $n>5040$, exactly when $\RH$ is true.  Taking logarithms turns the
comparison into the sign of
\[
 \M(n)=\log\A(n)-\euler-\log\log\log n.
\]
The proof is consequently a search for structural reasons why this margin is
negative, or, if $\RH$ fails, why it must sometimes be positive.

\subsection{Why the extremal integers suffice}

For a positive penalty $\eps$, the quantity
\[
 Q_\eps(m)=\A(m)m^{-\eps}
\]
rewards divisors but charges for size.  Its maximizers are the colossally
abundant integers.  They are useful because the penalty separates into
independent prime-power decisions: include a prime-power event when its
marginal gain exceeds its size cost, exclude it when the cost is larger.

Given a general integer $n$, choose the penalty
\[
 \eps=\frac{1}{\log n\,\log\log n}.
\]
Let $q$ maximize $Q_\eps$.  Maximality says that the logarithmic abundancy of
$n$ lies below the affine line determined by $q$ and this penalty.  The
function appearing in Robin's bound is concave, and the chosen penalty is its
tangent slope at $n$.  The tangent inequality therefore transfers Robin's
inequality from $q$ back to $n$.  A short exact calculation at the transition
$5040\leftrightarrow55440$ ensures that the chosen maximizer lies above the
exceptional cutoff whenever this reduction is used.  The bounded interval
not covered by that argument is settled separately.  This explains why
checking colossally abundant integers is both sufficient and necessary.

\subsection{Why the Riemann hypothesis implies the inequality}

Write $H=\log n$.  The prime-power factorization of $n$ expresses
$\log\A(n)$ as a sum of marginal gains.  Completing this sum to a convenient
set of small primes produces two competing quantities.  One is an analytic
error term, controlled through weighted estimates for the Chebyshev
functions $\vartheta$ and $\psi$.  The other is a definite arithmetic
deduction: an integer of height $H$ cannot take every small-prime gain for
free, so omitted primes or finite exponents impose a cost.

Under $\RH$, the weighted explicit formula bounds the analytic error on the
natural square-root scale
\[
 S(H)=H^{-1/2}(\log H)^{-1}.
\]
For $H\ge74500$, the proof shows that the total error is strictly less than
$(113/50)S(H)$, while a complete block of prime squares guarantees a cost of
at least $(113/50)S(H)$.  Hence error minus cost is negative, which is exactly
$\M(n)<0$.

The square-root scale has a simple spectral interpretation.  The explicit
formula rewrites the weighted prime-counting discrepancy as a superposition
of contributions resembling $H^\rho$, one for each nontrivial zero $\rho$ of
zeta.  Under $\RH$, every contribution has magnitude on the $H^{1/2}$ scale;
the weight damps the oscillation and the inverse-square zero sum controls the
aggregate amplitude.  The displayed error term is therefore a rigorous
envelope for all of those waves, not a numerical fit.

The prime cost is the complementary combinatorial picture.  An integer with
logarithmic size $H$ has a finite budget of prime exponents.  It cannot collect
every favorable small-prime contribution without spending that budget.  The
minimum in \eqref{eq:prime-cost} records whichever of two unavoidable losses
is cheaper for a prime.  The strict coefficient gap says that even this
conservative total loss is larger than the worst analytic envelope.

Below that analytic height, the argument is finite but not a decimal
computation.  Exact factorizations handle the first interval.  The remaining
range is covered by rational log-height rows.  Each row lists a finite box of
possible profitable prime powers and proves, by exact integer products and
rational logarithm bounds, that the Robin margin is negative throughout the
whole interval.  Thus the finite and analytic halves meet at $H=74500$
without an unverified numerical gap.

\subsection{Why the inequality implies the Riemann hypothesis}

The converse is best read contrapositively.  If $\RH$ is false, symmetry of
the zeta function supplies a nontrivial zero to the right of the critical
line.  That zero becomes a pole of a Mellin transform built from a weighted
prime-counting error.  Landau's positivity principle says that this transform
cannot remain analytic as far as that pole if the underlying error is
eventually of one sign.  Consequently the error has negative excursions of a
fixed power size arbitrarily far out.

Geometrically, the real axis of convergence acts like a wall.  Eventual
one-sidedness turns the Mellin transform, after $x=e^y$, into the Laplace
transform of a positive measure.  Positivity forces convergence to advance
whenever the transform analytically crosses that wall.  An off-critical zero,
however, creates a genuine pole just beyond it.  The two statements are
incompatible, so the assumed one-sided tail must fail repeatedly.  Section
\ref{sec:oscillation} replaces this picture by the exact continuation,
integrability, and pole calculations.

Nicolas's finite product $F(P)$ converts those analytic excursions into
negative excursions of $\log F(P)$.  To obtain actual integers, take
\[
 L_P=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,P).
\]
This least common multiple contains precisely the prime powers up to $P$ and
satisfies $\log L_P=\psi(P)$.  An exact identity expresses its Robin margin
as
\[
 \M(L_P)=-\log F(P)-D(P)-T(P),
\]
where the height loss $D(P)$ and the finite-prime-power reserve $T(P)$ are
nonnegative but smaller than the oscillation.  The negative excursions of
$\log F(P)$ therefore give positive values of $\M(L_P)$ for arbitrarily
large $P$.  These are violations of Robin's inequality above $5040$.
Hence universal validity of the inequality forces $\RH$.

The technical proof that follows adds the exact thresholds, constants,
convergence arguments, and finite data needed to make this outline rigorous;
it does not change the four-step logical route.

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two formal interfaces merit a more precise explanation.  First, a classical
sum over zeta zeros is normally understood to repeat a zero according to its
analytic multiplicity.  That convention is not enough for the interchanges
needed in Lean.  The multiplicity-aware infrastructure supplies an explicit
index type for the divisor of $\xi$, a value map from indices to nonzero zeros,
inverse-norm summability, and the hypotheses licensing each exchange of an
integral with an infinite sum.  It proves no new fact about where the zeros
lie; it turns the classical convention into a reusable formal interface.

Second, the complete prime cost in \eqref{eq:prime-cost} is a
bookkeeping device.  Robin estimates several prime products separately.  The
present proof first sums the actual exponent choices of an arbitrary integer,
then completes to all relevant primes.  Each missing prime or prematurely
terminated exponent pays the smaller of two explicit penalties, so the
analytic error and arithmetic loss can be bounded in independent modules.
This modularity is useful for verification, but no theorem here says that it
is computationally minimal or analytically more efficient than every earlier
argument.

Third, Lemma~\ref{lem:nicolas-comparison} is called a ``finite Nicolas
comparison'' because it connects Nicolas's finite product to the analytic
error used in his oscillation theorem.  The product, Mertens series, and
partial-summation idea are classical.  The repository-owned contribution is
the exact identity
$\log F=R_M+R_\vartheta+K$, the explicit $4/x$ tail, and the separately proved
signs $R_\vartheta\le0$ and $K\le J$ that make the comparison usable without
asymptotic shorthand.  This is again a formal reconstruction and reusable
interface, not a new oscillation phenomenon.

\subsection{Relation to prior formal work}

The adjacent formal literature concerns the analytic objects around the

Gomes and Kontorovich~\cite{GomesKontorovich2020} formalized an equivalent

Those developments establish definitions and reusable analytic foundations.
The present artifact connects them to a different endpoint: Robin's exact
sum-of-divisors criterion, including the weighted endpoint, complete exponent
budget, finite certificate cover, Nicolas--Landau continuation, and LCM
transfer.  The author is not aware of an earlier mechanically checked proof of
this complete biconditional, but no exhaustive priority claim is made.  The
comparison asserted here is narrower and inspectable: none of the cited formal

The primary intended audience is therefore formal mathematics and analytic
number theory.  For AI-assisted mathematics, the contribution is a concrete

\section{Arithmetic preliminaries}

For $n>e^e$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
  \M(n)=\log \A(n)-\euler-\log\log\log n.
\end{equation}
All logarithms in \eqref{eq:margin} are then defined, and Robin's inequality
is equivalent to $\M(n)<0$.

Write $n=\prod_p p^{a_p}$.  For a prime $p$ and an integer $j\ge1$, define
the marginal gain and threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:event}
  g_{p,j}
  =\log\!\left(\frac{1-p^{-(j+1)}}{1-p^{-j}}\right),
  \qquad
  \tau_{p,j}=\frac{g_{p,j}}{\log p}.
\end{equation}
The event $(p,j)$ records the change from exponent $j-1$ to exponent $j$.

\begin{lemma}[Prime-power event decomposition]\label{lem:event-decomp}
For every positive integer $n$ and every $\eps>0$,
\begin{align}
  \log \A(n)
    &=\sum_p\sum_{1\le j\le a_p}g_{p,j},\label{eq:event-abundancy}\\
  \log Q_{\eps}(n)
    &=\sum_p\sum_{1\le j\le a_p}
       \bigl(g_{p,j}-\eps\log p\bigr).\label{eq:event-ca}
\end{align}
For fixed $p$, the sequences $g_{p,j}$ and $\tau_{p,j}$ are nonincreasing in
$j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The geometric-series identity gives
\[
  \frac{\sig(p^a)}{p^a}
  =\frac{1-p^{-(a+1)}}{1-p^{-1}}
  =\prod_{j=1}^{a}
    \frac{1-p^{-(j+1)}}{1-p^{-j}}.
\]
Multiplicativity and logarithms give \eqref{eq:event-abundancy}; subtracting
$\eps\log n$ gives \eqref{eq:event-ca}.  If $q=p^{-1}\in(0,1)$, direct
cross-multiplication shows that
$(1-q^{j+2})/(1-q^{j+1})\le(1-q^{j+1})/(1-q^j)$.
Taking logarithms and then dividing by $\log p>0$ proves the monotonicity.
\end{proof}

The reduced weight of an event at parameter $\eps$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-weight}
  w_{\eps}(p,j)=g_{p,j}-\eps\log p
               =(\tau_{p,j}-\eps)\log p.
\end{equation}
Thus events above the threshold have nonnegative reduced weight and events
below it have nonpositive reduced weight.  This is the elementary
complementary-slackness description of a colossally abundant factorization.

\begin{lemma}[Existence of a maximizer]\label{lem:ca-exists}
For every $\eps>0$ there exists an integer $N>1$ maximizing $Q_{\eps}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The divisor bound
\[
  \A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\frac1d
  \le \sum_{1\le d\le n}\frac1d
  \le 1+\log n
\]
implies
$Q_{\eps}(n)\le(1+\log n)n^{-\eps}\to0$.  Beyond a finite point the
objective is below its value at $2$.  A maximum over the remaining finite
set is therefore a global maximum.
\end{proof}

\section{The colossally abundant tangent reduction}\label{sec:extremal}

Put $H_n=\log n$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda}
  \lambda(n)=\frac1{H_n\log H_n}.
\end{equation}
This is the derivative at $H_n$ of the concave function
$\phi(H)=\log\log H$.

\begin{lemma}[Global tangent inequality]\label{lem:tangent}
If $L,U>1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
  \log\log U-\log\log L
  \le \frac{U-L}{L\log L}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply $\log y\le y-1$ first to
$y=(\log U)/(\log L)$ and then to $y=U/L$.  Division by the positive
quantities $L$ and $\log L$ gives \eqref{eq:tangent}.  This proof applies
whether $U$ lies to the left or to the right of $L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tangent transfer]\label{prop:tangent-transfer}
Let $n,q>5040$.  Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-objective}
  \log Q_{\lambda(n)}(n)
  \le \log Q_{\lambda(n)}(q).
\end{equation}
If Robin's inequality holds at $q$, then it holds at $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $L=H_n$ and $U=H_q$, inequality
\eqref{eq:tangent-objective} becomes
\[
  \log\A(n)-\lambda(n)L
  \le \log\A(q)-\lambda(n)U.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent},
$\phi(U)-\phi(L)\le\lambda(n)(U-L)$.  Adding the two inequalities yields
\[
  \log\A(n)-\phi(L)
  \le \log\A(q)-\phi(U).
\]
After subtracting $\euler$, the left- and right-hand sides are precisely
$\M(n)$ and $\M(q)$.  Hence $\M(q)<0$ implies $\M(n)<0$.
\end{proof}

The remaining point is to ensure that a maximizer at the tangent parameter
is itself above the exceptional cutoff.  The exact startup transition is
particularly useful.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon-zero}
  \eps_0=\frac{\log(12/11)}{\log 11}.
\end{equation}
The event $(11,1)$ changes $5040$ into $55440=11\cdot5040$ and has threshold
$\eps_0$.

\begin{lemma}[The startup tie]\label{lem:startup-tie}
Both $5040$ and $55440$ maximize $Q_{\eps_0}$.  Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon-enclosure}
  \frac1{34}<\eps_0<\frac1{22}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The factorization $5040=2^4 3^2 5\cdot7$ determines its included and first
excluded events.  Put
\[
  r_{p,j}=\frac{1-p^{-(j+1)}}{1-p^{-j}},
  \qquad \tau_{p,j}=\frac{\log r_{p,j}}{\log p}.
\]
The complete boundary calculation is small enough to display:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccl@{}}
\toprule
event & $r_{p,j}$ & comparison with $\eps_0$ \\
\midrule
$(2,4)$ & $31/30$ & $\tau_{2,4}>\eps_0$ \\
$(3,2)$ & $13/12$ & $\tau_{3,2}>\eps_0$ \\
$(5,1)$ & $6/5$   & $\tau_{5,1}>\eps_0$ \\
$(7,1)$ & $8/7$   & $\tau_{7,1}>\eps_0$ \\
\midrule
$(2,5)$ & $63/62$ & $\tau_{2,5}<\eps_0$ \\
$(3,3)$ & $40/39$ & $\tau_{3,3}<\eps_0$ \\
$(5,2)$ & $31/30$ & $\tau_{5,2}<\eps_0$ \\
$(7,2)$ & $57/56$ & $\tau_{7,2}<\eps_0$ \\
$(11,1)$ & $12/11$ & $\tau_{11,1}=\eps_0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here is the complete arithmetic behind the strict signs.  For an event with
base $p$ and gain ratio $r$, positivity of logarithms gives
\[
 p<r^N\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac1N<\frac{\log r}{\log p},
 \qquad
 r^N<p\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{\log r}{\log p}<\frac1N.
\]
The cutoff enclosure~\eqref{eq:epsilon-enclosure} follows from the two exact
rational inequalities
\[
 11<(12/11)^{34},\qquad (12/11)^{22}<11.
\]
For the four included top events the kernel checks
\[
 2<(31/30)^{22},\quad 3<(13/12)^{22},\quad
 5<(6/5)^{22},\quad 7<(8/7)^{22};
\]
hence their thresholds exceed $1/22>\eps_0$.  For the four first-missing
events it checks
\[
 (63/62)^{34}<2,\quad (40/39)^{34}<3,\quad
 (31/30)^{34}<5,\quad (57/56)^{34}<7;
\]
hence their thresholds lie below $1/34<\eps_0$.  These are integer
comparisons after denominators are cleared; the tie $(11,1)$ is definitional.

For a fixed prime, monotonicity in $j$ propagates the displayed top-event
and first-missing-event signs through every layer.  Every prime not dividing
$5040$ is at least $11$, and the first-layer threshold is nonincreasing in
the prime; hence its reduced weight is nonpositive, with equality only at
$(11,1)$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced-weight} now compares the event packet
of $5040$ term by term with every competitor.  Thus $5040$ maximizes
$Q_{\eps_0}$.  The tied event has weight zero, and adjoining it multiplies
$5040$ by $11$, proving the same maximality for $55440$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:maximizer-above}
If $n\ge720720$ and $q$ maximizes $Q_{\lambda(n)}$, then $q>5040$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The function $\lambda(n)$ is nonincreasing above $5040$.  Exact logarithmic
bounds give
\[
  \lambda(720720)<\frac1{34}<\eps_0,
\]
and hence $\lambda(n)<\eps_0$.  Write $\lambda=\lambda(n)$.  If
$q\le5040$, then maximality at $\eps_0$ gives
\[
 \log Q_\lambda(q)-\log Q_\lambda(5040)
 =\log Q_{\eps_0}(q)-\log Q_{\eps_0}(5040)
  +(\eps_0-\lambda)\log\frac q{5040}\le0.
\]
On the other hand, the exact tie of Lemma~\ref{lem:startup-tie} gives
\[
 \log Q_\lambda(55440)-\log Q_\lambda(5040)
   =(\eps_0-\lambda)\log 11>0.
\]
Thus no $q\le5040$ can maximize $Q_\lambda$, because $55440$ has strictly
larger objective value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reduction to colossally abundant integers]
\label{prop:ca-reduction}
Assume Robin's inequality for $5040<n<720720$.  Then the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Robin's inequality holds for every $n>5040$;
  \item it holds for every colossally abundant $N>5040$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i) needs proof.  Let $n\ge720720$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:ca-exists}, choose a maximizer $q$ for
$Q_{\lambda(n)}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:maximizer-above} gives $q>5040$, so (ii)
gives Robin's inequality at $q$.  Maximality gives
\eqref{eq:tangent-objective}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent-transfer}
transfers the inequality to $n$.  The assumed finite interval handles the
remaining integers.
\end{proof}

\section{The finite range}\label{sec:finite}

The analytic argument in Section~\ref{sec:rh-forward} starts at
$\log n=74500$.  The exact cutoff therefore requires a proof for all
$5040<n$ below that logarithmic height.  We divide it into three pieces.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:small-factor}
For $5041\le n<7560$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:127-35}
  35\sig(n)\le127n.
\end{equation}
Consequently Robin's inequality holds throughout this interval.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each integer in the interval, an exact prime-power factorization is
given.  Pairwise coprimality and the identity
$\sig(p^a)=1+p+\cdots+p^a$ reduce \eqref{eq:127-35} to natural-number
arithmetic.  To pass from \eqref{eq:127-35} to Robin's inequality, use the
exact bounds
\[
  e^{\euler}>\frac{177}{100},
  \qquad
  \log\log n>\frac{103}{50},
  \qquad
  \frac{127}{35}<\frac{177}{100}\frac{103}{50}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:startup-tangent}
Robin's inequality holds for $7560\le n<720720$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The maximality of $5040$ at $\eps_0$ gives
\[
  \log\A(n)-\eps_0\log n
  \le \log\A(5040)-\eps_0\log5040.
\]
Set $y=\log n$.  It is enough to prove positivity of the explicit gap
\[
 G(y)=\euler+\log\log y-\log\A(5040)
      -\eps_0\bigl(y-\log5040\bigr),
\]
because the preceding inequality then places $\log\A(n)$ strictly below
$\euler+\log\log y$.  The curvature used at this step is explicit:
\[
 G''(y)=-\frac{\log y+1}{y^2(\log y)^2}<0\qquad(y>1).
\]
Thus $G$ is concave, and its value at every point of a closed interval is at
least the smaller of its two endpoint values.  The endpoint checks use the
following strict rational bounds:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cp{.72\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
endpoint & certified inequalities \\
\midrule
$7560$ & $\euler>577/1000$, $\log\log\log7560>3917/5000$,
  $\log\A(5040)<269/200$, and
  $\eps_0\log(3/2)<203/13500$ \\
$720720$ & $\euler>72/125$, $\log\log\log720720>191/200$,
  $\log\A(5040)<269/200$, and $\eps_0\log143<5/27$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\A(5040)=403/105$, and the arguments of the last two logarithms arise
from $7560/5040=3/2$ and $720720/5040=143$.  Substitution makes each endpoint
gap a strict rational inequality, so concavity proves positivity throughout
the interval.
\end{proof}

For the much larger interval above $720720$, a compact rational certificate
replaces integer-by-integer factorization.  A certificate row consists of a
log-height interval $[L,U]$, a rational slope $\lambda$, prime-layer cutoffs,
an upper bound for the corresponding product, a lower height bound, and
lower bounds for $\log L$ and $\log U$.  The layer inequalities certify which
prime-power events can have positive reduced weight.  The product inequality
bounds the maximum possible value of
$\log\A(n)-\lambda\log n$.  Finally, concavity reduces the Robin comparison
on $[L,U]$ to two endpoint inequalities.

More precisely, for a cutoff vector $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_d)$ define the layer box
\[
 \mathcal B(c)=\{(p,j):p\ \hbox{prime},\ 1\le j\le d,\ p\le c_j\}.
\]
The reduced weight of an event is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-reduced-weight}
 w_{p,j}(\lambda)=g_{p,j}-\lambda\log p
 =\log p\,(\tau_{p,j}-\lambda).
\end{equation}
To state the integer boundary checks without pretending that a cutoff is
prime, extend this expression to every real base $x>1$ by
\[
 \widetilde w_j(x;\lambda)=
 \log\!\left(1+\frac{x-1}{x(x^j-1)}\right)-\lambda\log x.
\]
For a prime $p$, this is exactly $w_{p,j}(\lambda)$.  For every layer $j$,
the row proves
\[
 \widetilde w_j(c_j;\lambda)\ge0,
 \qquad \widetilde w_j(c_j+1;\lambda)\le0,
\]
using the rational logarithm bounds below.  The gain decreases with the base,
so these two boundary signs classify every prime in that layer.  The extra
check $\widetilde w_{d+1}(2;\lambda)\le0$, together with monotonicity in the
layer and base, excludes every event above the stored vector.  Thus events inside
$\mathcal B(c)$ have nonnegative weight and all events outside have
nonpositive weight.  Any integer's actual exponent packet is consequently
bounded above by the sum over this finite box; this is the precise sense in
which the box is a valid worst case.

Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} prints the complete first row, the scaled
logarithm checker, and the two endpoint predicates.  Appendix
\ref{app:cover-inventory} lists all 36 intervals and their main structural
parameters.  Keeping those arithmetic audits separate here leaves the main
argument focused on the row schema and its soundness theorem.

The logarithm certificates used here are elementary and explicit.  For a
rational $q\ge1$, put $z=(q-1)/(q+1)$.  Then $0\le z<1$ and, for $N\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 L_N(q)&=2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2i+1}}{2i+1},\notag\\
 U_N(q)&=L_N(q)+\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{1-z^2},\label{eq:log-series}\\
 L_N(q)&\le\log q\le U_N(q).\notag
\end{align}
The upper remainder is deliberately coarse: it follows by deleting the
denominators $2i+1$ from the positive tail of the atanh series.  Consequently
both bounds in \eqref{eq:log-series} are rational and their soundness is proved
once for arbitrary $q$ and $N$.  Every row in this proof uses $N=20$.

\paragraph{Certificate discovery versus certificate checking.}
A practical way to find a row is to choose rational $L,U,\lambda$ and then,
for layer $j$, choose an integer cutoff $c_j$.  The checker compares the
threshold formula at $c_j$ and $c_j+1$: the former must have nonnegative
reduced weight and the latter nonpositive reduced weight.  Monotonicity in
the base then proves the signs for every prime in that layer.  A final check
at base $2$ proves that no still higher layer can contribute positively.
From the resulting finite box one computes the exact integer products
\[
A=\prod(p^{j+1}-1),\qquad
B=\prod p(p^j-1),\qquad
Q=\prod p.
\]
All three products are over $\mathcal B(c)$.  Their logarithms give exactly
\[
\sum_{(p,j)\in\mathcal B(c)}w_{p,j}(\lambda)
 =\log A-\log B-\lambda\log Q.
\]
For example, Row~00 has $c=[17,5,3,2,2]$.  Its layers contain respectively
the prime bases
\[
 \{2,3,5,7,11,13,17\},\quad \{2,3,5\},\quad
 \{2,3\},\quad \{2\},\quad \{2\}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\begin{align*}
 A&=8\,490\,305\,136\,604\,741\,632\,000,\qquad
 B=1\,651\,300\,226\,181\,365\,760\,000,\\
 Q&=367\,567\,200.
\end{align*}
The kernel then verifies, among the remaining row predicates,
\[
 A\le\frac{5141587823941}{10^{12}}B,
 \qquad
 2^{28}\frac{11486475}{8388608}\le Q.
\]
Rational bounds of the forms $A\le(\text{gain upper})B$ and
$2^e m\le Q$, together with \eqref{eq:log-series}, produce the objective and
height bounds.  If either endpoint inequality fails, one changes the slope
or shortens the interval; accepted endpoints are then chained exactly.
This search is only certificate discovery.  It need not be trusted or even
reproduced: the stored row contains all of its outputs, the general soundness
lemma proves what successful checks imply, and Lean's kernel evaluates every
row predicate from those visible rational and integer values.

The discovery process was iterative, not an optimization computation.  An
earlier checked cover contained 39 rows.  The cancellation-aware coefficient
revision in \commitlink{208b44750090b7b0e2254e3d511b4d119052cd03}{208b447}
lowered the analytic handoff from $100000$ to $74500$ and removed the last
three rows, leaving the present 36-row witness.  Rejected candidate rows were
not retained as a stable trace, so no numerical count of trial proposals is
claimed.  Nor is 36 claimed minimal: it is an upper bound supplied by one
accepted cover, and there is no nontrivial lower bound or optimality theorem
for this certificate format.  Different slopes, interval widths, or
logarithm approximants could change the row count without changing the
soundness argument.

The historical discovery runs were not instrumented per candidate, so the
paper does not report invented search times or a trial count.  Appendix
\ref{app:cover-inventory} instead reports a reproducible quantity: the elapsed
time for each accepted row module in one identified clean CI build.  Those
times measure elaboration and kernel checking on that runner, not intrinsic
certificate complexity; they vary with hardware, toolchain, and cache state.

The height data certify that the relevant prime-base product is at least
$2^e m$; the checker turns this into a lower logarithmic height with a
20-term rational log series.  The gain bound is likewise converted into an
upper logarithmic objective.  Thus this row checks the two literal rational
inequalities displayed in Lemma~\ref{lem:row-sound}, not floating-point
approximations.  Its complete definition and kernel proof are
\codefile{Robin1984/Finite/Certificates/Row00.lean}.  The other rows have the
same schema; the full list and gap-free cover proof are
\codefile{Robin1984/Finite/Certificates/AllRows.lean}, with all individual
records in \codedir{Robin1984/Finite/Certificates}.

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of a finite row]\label{lem:row-sound}
Suppose a row $[L,U]$ satisfies its layer-sign, product, height, and endpoint
inequalities.  If $n>5040$ and $L\le\log n\le U$, then $\M(n)<0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sign checks and \eqref{eq:reduced-weight} bound the actual factorization
packet by the finite threshold box.  The exact product bounds convert that
box into an upper bound $B$ for
$\log\A(n)-\lambda\log n$.  The two endpoint checks state
\[
  B<\euler+\log\log L-\lambda L,
  \qquad
  B<\euler+\log\log U-\lambda U.
\]
Concavity of $y\mapsto\log\log y-\lambda y$ gives the same strict inequality
at $y=\log n$.  Rearrangement is $\M(n)<0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified finite cover]\label{prop:finite-cover}
Robin's inequality holds whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-log-range}
  720720\le n,
  \qquad
  \log n<74500.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Thirty-six rational rows form a gap-free chain from $336/25$ to
$37283397387/500000$, an endpoint strictly larger than $74500$.  Since
$336/25<\log720720$, they cover \eqref{eq:finite-log-range}.  Every row
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:row-sound}.  In the final retained
row the first-layer product includes every prime at most $70849$; it is split
into 105 contiguous blocks, whose exact products concatenate to the required
prefix.  The blocks cover the half-open base range $[0,70850)$ without a gap,
and each block proves its numerator, denominator, and prime-base product.
Concatenation therefore reconstructs the same first-layer factors in $A,B,Q$
that a monolithic product would contain; the split changes evaluation cost,
not the mathematical packet.
All comparisons are integer or rational inequalities.  Thus the list of rows
and their checks constitute a finite proof of the proposition.
\end{proof}

Combining Propositions~\ref{prop:small-factor},
\ref{prop:startup-tangent}, and~\ref{prop:finite-cover} gives the following.

\begin{theorem}[Finite completion]\label{thm:finite}
If $5040<n$ and $\log n<74500$, then Robin's inequality holds.
\end{theorem}

In particular, Proposition~\ref{prop:ca-reduction} is now unconditional.

\section{The implication under the Riemann hypothesis}
\label{sec:rh-forward}

Let
\[
  \vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p,
  \qquad
  \psi(x)=\sum_{p^k\le x}\log p.
\]
The analytic estimate is most transparent after setting $H=\log n$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:natural-scale}
  S(H)=H^{-1/2}(\log H)^{-1}.
\end{equation}

The section is easiest read backward from its last line.  We seek
$\M(n)<0$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:budget} bounds this margin by an analytic
debit $\E(H)$ minus an arithmetic credit $\Pcost(H)$.  The weighted explicit
formula controls the debit; a complete block of prime squares supplies the
credit; and Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-gap} proves that, once
$H\ge74500$, the credit is at least $(113/50)S(H)$ while the debit is
strictly smaller.  The formulas below establish these three statements but
do not add another logical layer.

For a first reading, Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-ef} may be used as the analytic
input and its kernel-estimate proof skipped; the narrative resumes at the
definition of $\Pcost(H)$ and $\E(H)$.  The intervening integration-by-parts
calculation is retained because it explains the exact constant $c_0$, the
asymmetric error bound, and the otherwise delicate endpoint $m=1$.

We first record the weighted explicit-formula input.  For $m\ge1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
  w_m(t)=t^{-m-1}\frac{m\log t+1}{(\log t)^2},
  \qquad
  I_m(x)=\int_x^{\infty}(\psi(t)-t)w_m(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
For a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$, counted with multiplicity, put
\[
  K_m(\rho,x)=\int_x^{\infty}
    t^{\rho-m-1}\frac{m\log t+1}{(\log t)^2}\,dt.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Robin's weighted explicit formula]\label{lem:weighted-ef}
Assume $\RH$.  For $m\ge1$ and $x\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-ef}
  I_m(x)=-\sum_{\rho}\frac{K_m(\rho,x)}{\rho}-T_m(x),
\end{equation}
where the pole, gamma-factor, and trivial-zero correction is the explicit
nonnegative integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trivial-correction}
 T_m(x)=\int_x^\infty w_m(t)
 \left(\log(2\pi)+\frac12\log(1-t^{-2})\right)\,dt.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge2$ the parenthesis lies between $0$ and $\log(2\pi)$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trivial-correction-bound}
 0\le T_m(x)\le
 \frac{\log(2\pi)x^{-m}}{\log x}.
\end{equation}
If
\[
  c_0=\euler+2-\log(4\pi),
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-bound}
 -B_m(x)-\frac{\log(2\pi)x^{-m}}{\log x}
 \le I_m(x)\le B_m(x),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bmx}
 B_m(x)=c_0x^{1/2-m}(\log x)^{-1}
 \left(m+(\log x)^{-1}
 +\frac{4}{(2m-1)(\log x)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the explicit formula to the continuous cutoff whose Mellin transform
is $K_m(s,x)/s$.  The arithmetic side is the von Mangoldt sum associated
with $I_m(x)$; moving the vertical line separates the pole at $1$, the
nontrivial zeros, and the negative even zeros.  Every interchange is
justified by an explicit summable norm majorant, so the zero sum retains
analytic multiplicity rather than choosing an enumeration without proof.

The kernel calculation is not a black box.  Since
\[
 -\frac{d}{dt}\frac{t^{-m}}{\log t}
 =t^{-m-1}\frac{m\log t+1}{(\log t)^2},
\]
one integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-first-parts}
 \frac{K_m(\rho,x)}{\rho}
 =\frac{x^{\rho-m}}{\rho\log x}
  \int_x^\infty\frac{t^{\rho-m-1}}{\log t}\,dt.
\end{equation}
A second integration by parts, with
$d(t^{\rho-m})=(\rho-m)t^{\rho-m-1}\,dt$, separates the leading term
\[
 \frac{m}{\rho(m-\rho)}
 \frac{x^{\rho-m}}{\log x}
\]
from a remainder containing $(\log t)^{-2}$.  Bounding that remainder once
more by integration against $(\log t)^{-3}$ gives, under
$\RH$ and hence $\Re\rho=1/2$, the pointwise majorant
\[
 \left|\frac{K_m(\rho,x)}{\rho}\right|
 \le \frac1{|\rho|^2}\,
 x^{1/2-m}(\log x)^{-1}
 \left(m+(\log x)^{-1}
       +\frac4{(2m-1)(\log x)^2}\right).
\]
Hadamard factorization of the completed zeta function supplies the exact
multiplicity-counted identity
\[
  \sum_{\rho}|\rho|^{-2}=\euler+2-\log(4\pi)=c_0.
\]
Summing the majorant gives $B_m(x)$.  The combined pole/trivial-zero factor
is exactly \eqref{eq:trivial-correction}; its integrand bound and
$\int_x^\infty w_m(t)\,dt=x^{-m}/\log x$ prove
\eqref{eq:trivial-correction-bound}, explaining the asymmetry in
\eqref{eq:weighted-bound}.

The contour identity initially covers $m\ge2$.  For the endpoint let
\[
 q(t)=\frac{t(\log t+1)}{2\log t+1},\qquad
 q'(t)=\frac{\log t(2\log t+3)}{(2\log t+1)^2}.
\]
Exact integration by parts gives
\[
 I_1(x)=q(x)I_2(x)+\int_x^\infty q'(t)I_2(t)\,dt.
\]
The same identity is proved separately for the zero series and for
$T_m$, with absolute-integrability bounds licensing each interchange.
Substitution of the $m=2$ formula therefore yields \eqref{eq:weighted-ef}
at $m=1$, rather than obtaining it by a limiting convention.  This is
Robin's Lemma~2 with no omitted endpoint.
\end{proof}

Costa Pereira's estimates for $\psi-\vartheta$, with the corrected constants
from~\cite{CostaPereira1987}, control the prime-power part separated from
\eqref{eq:weighted-ef}.  Abel summation supplies the complementary lower
bound for a complete block of prime squares.

Define the complete prime cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-cost}
  \Pcost(H)=\sum_{p\le H/2}
  \min\!\left\{\frac{\log p}{H\log H},\frac1{p^2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Also define
\begin{align}
  \E(H)={}&(2+c_0)\frac{H^{-1/2}}{\log H}
  +(c_0-2)\frac{H^{-1/2}}{(\log H)^2}\notag\\
  &+(8+4c_0)\frac{H^{-1/2}}{(\log H)^3}
  +2\frac{H^{-2/3}}{\log H}
  +\log(2\pi)\frac{H^{-1}}{\log H}.
  \label{eq:mertens-scalar}
\end{align}
Thus $\E(H)$ is a uniform upper bound assembled from the weighted
prime-function errors, whereas $\Pcost(H)$ is a nonnegative deduction forced
by completing the exponent sum to all small primes.  Their names record
their roles in \eqref{eq:budget}; neither is a new number-theoretic function
outside this proof.

\begin{proposition}[Complete exponent budget]\label{prop:budget}
Assume $\RH$.  If $n\ge1$ and $H=\log n\ge20000$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  \M(n)\le \E(H)-\Pcost(H).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a prime $p$ occurring in $n$ with exponent $a$, the telescoping identity
from Lemma~\ref{lem:event-decomp} and $\log y\le y-1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-gain}
 \log\frac{1-p^{-(a+1)}}{1-p^{-1}}
 \le \frac1p-p^{-(a+1)}-m_p,
 \qquad
 m_p=\log(1-p^{-1})+p^{-1}\le0.
\end{equation}
Subtract $a\log p/(H\log H)$ from each prime contribution.  Completing the
sum to all primes $p\le H$ does not lose the omitted primes: if $a=0$ and
$p\le H/2$, the benchmark contribution pays at least the minimum in
\eqref{eq:prime-cost}.  Summation therefore yields a prime-reciprocal
expression minus $\Pcost(H)$.

Partial summation writes the prime-reciprocal expression as
$\log\log H$ plus the Meissel--Mertens constant and a weighted
$\vartheta$-error.
Replacing $\vartheta$ by $\psi$ separates the nonnegative prime-power tail.
Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-ef} and the Costa Pereira bounds show that the
remaining weighted error is at most \eqref{eq:mertens-scalar}.  The Mertens
constants cancel with the sum of the $m_p$, giving exactly
\eqref{eq:budget}.
\end{proof}

The numerical heart of the large-height proof is the following strict gap.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit coefficient gap]\label{prop:coefficient-gap}
Assume $\RH$.  For $H\ge74500$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-coefficients}
  \E(H)<\frac{113}{50}S(H),
  \qquad
  \Pcost(H)\ge\frac{113}{50}S(H).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first inequality, divide \eqref{eq:mertens-scalar} by $S(H)$.
The exact identity becomes
\[
  2+c_0+(c_0-2)(\log H)^{-1}
  +(8+4c_0)(\log H)^{-2}
  +2H^{-1/6}+\log(2\pi)H^{-1/2}.
\]
Uniform rational bounds valid for every $H\ge74500$ control
$(\log H)^{-1}$, $H^{-1/6}$, $H^{-1/2}$, $c_0$, and $\log(2\pi)$.
The values actually used are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-rational-data}
\begin{aligned}
 \log H&\ge\frac{56}{5}, &
 (\log H)^{-1}&\le\frac5{56}, &
 (\log H)H^{-1/6}&\le\frac{87}{50},\\
 H^{-1/2}&\le\frac1{272}, &
 c_0&\le\frac1{20}, &
 \log(2\pi)&\le\frac{46}{25}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There is no numerical conflict in the bound for $c_0$:
\[
 c_0=\euler+2-\log(4\pi)
     =0.0461914179\ldots < 0.05=\frac1{20}.
\]
The proof does not depend on that decimal evaluation.  The harmonic-number
and logarithm bounds in Appendix~\ref{app:coefficients} prove
$c_0<154/3125<1/20$ rationally.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:error-rational-data}, while retaining the favorable negative
coefficient $c_0-2$, gives the exact margin
\[
 \frac{\E(H)}{S(H)}\le\frac{3010451}{1332800}
 =\frac{113}{50}-\frac{1677}{1332800}<\frac{113}{50}.
\]

For the second inequality, take the complete prime-square block between
$\sqrt{2H}$ and $H/2$.  Abel summation gives a dominant normalized main term
tending to $2\sqrt2$, while the prime-function error, endpoint, and
prime-power terms are lower order.  The calculation retains the RH error at
the far endpoint instead of replacing the whole $\vartheta$ tail by a coarse
global bound; only after that cancellation is the remaining nonnegative
prime-power contribution discarded.  Appendix~\ref{app:coefficients} gives
the complete normalized expression and every rational substitution.  The
resulting exact lower margin is
\[
 \frac{\Pcost(H)}{S(H)}\ge
 \frac{15537277889}{6868000000}
 =\frac{113}{50}+\frac{15597889}{6868000000}>
 \frac{113}{50}.
\]
Thus the two sides of the gap are proved independently and meet with positive
rational margins.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The RH implication]\label{thm:rh-forward}
If $\RH$ holds, then Robin's inequality holds for every $n>5040$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\log n<74500$, use Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.  Otherwise combine
Propositions~\ref{prop:budget} and~\ref{prop:coefficient-gap}:
\[
  \M(n)
  <\left(\frac{113}{50}-\frac{113}{50}\right)S(\log n)
  =0.
\]
This is equivalent to Robin's inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{The Nicolas--Landau oscillation}\label{sec:oscillation}

Define Nicolas's finite product and normalized function by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nicolas-function}
  \Pprod(x)=\prod_{p\le x}\left(1-\frac1p\right),
  \qquad
  F(x)=e^{\euler}\log\vartheta(x)\,\Pprod(x).
\end{equation}
The signed quantity used below is $\log F(x)$.

Let
\[
  k(t)=\frac{(\log t)^{-1}+(\log t)^{-2}}{t^2},
\]
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KJ}
 K(x)=\int_x^{\infty}(\vartheta(t)-t)k(t)\,dt,
 \qquad
 J(x)=\int_x^{\infty}(\psi(t)-t)k(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}

For functions $g,h:\R\to\R$, write $g=\Omega_-(h)$ at infinity if there
is $c>0$ such that, for every $X$, some $x\ge X$ satisfies
$g(x)\le-c h(x)$.

Only two outputs of this section are needed later.  First,
$\log F(x)$ is bounded above by the weighted prime error $J(x)$ plus a term
of order $x^{-1}$.  Second, if $\RH$ fails, Landau's principle forces
$J(x)$ below a negative multiple of $x^{-b}$ arbitrarily far out for some
$b<1/2$.  Since $x^{-1}=o(x^{-b})$, the first output transfers the second
one to $\log F$.  Section~\ref{sec:lcm} then absorbs the remaining LCM
losses, which are only of order $P^{-1/2}\log P$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite Nicolas comparison]\label{lem:nicolas-comparison}
For all sufficiently large $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nicolas-upper}
  \log F(x)\le J(x)+\frac4x.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact decomposition, with no asymptotic notation, is
\begin{align}
 \log F(x)={}&R_M(x)+R_\vartheta(x)+K(x),
 \label{eq:finite-nicolas-split}\\
 R_M(x)={}&
 \sum_{0<n\le\lfloor x\rfloor}
 \left(\log(1-n^{-1})+n^{-1}\right)-(M-\euler),\notag\\
 R_\vartheta(x)={}&
 \log\log\vartheta(x)-\log\log x
 -\frac{\vartheta(x)-x}{x\log x},\notag
\end{align}
where the summand is understood to be zero for nonprimes and $M$ is the
Meissel--Mertens constant in the convergent prime summand series.  The
explicit tail estimate gives $|R_M(x)|\le4/x$.  The inequality
$\log y\le y-1$, applied to the ratio of the two logarithmic heights, is
exactly $R_\vartheta(x)\le0$ after simplification.

Finally
\[
 J(x)-K(x)=\int_x^\infty
   \bigl(\psi(t)-\vartheta(t)\bigr)k(t)\,dt\ge0,
\]
because both factors are nonnegative.  Applying these three bounds to
\eqref{eq:finite-nicolas-split} gives \eqref{eq:nicolas-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Nicolas--Landau negative oscillation]
\label{prop:nicolas-landau}
If $\RH$ is false, then there is $b$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b-range}
  0<b<\frac12
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-omega}
  J(x)=\Omega_-(x^{-b}).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Failure of $\RH$, together with the functional equation and conjugation
symmetry, gives a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$ with
$1/2<\Re\rho<1$.  On its horizontal line choose a zero $\rho_*$ with
maximal real part.  Choose
\[
 1-\Re\rho_*<b<\frac12.
\]

The contradiction is an instance of a simple positivity obstruction.
If the corrected tail $J(x)+x^{-b}$ were eventually positive, its Mellin
transform would be the Laplace transform, under $x=e^y$, of a positive
measure.  Positivity makes convergence propagate to every real point that
the analytic continuation can cross.  The zeta logarithmic derivative,
however, gives that continuation a pole at $s=1-\rho_*$, strictly inside
the propagated half-plane.  The remainder of the proof makes each part of
this obstruction precise.

Suppose \eqref{eq:J-omega} fails.  Then, beyond some $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-tail}
  J(x)+x^{-b}>0.
\end{equation}
Define the positive measure
\[
  d\mu_b(x)=\boldsymbol{1}_{(X,\infty)}(x)
       x^{-1}\bigl(J(x)+x^{-b}\bigr)\,dx
\]
and use $Y(x)=\log x$ as the random variable.  Its complex
moment-generating function is the Mellin transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landau-mgf}
  M_b(s)=\int_X^\infty x^{s-1}\bigl(J(x)+x^{-b}\bigr)\,dx.
\end{equation}
For $\Re s<0$ the integral is computed directly.  Integration by parts in
the $J$ term expresses it through the Mellin transform of $\psi(x)-x$ and
hence through $-\zeta'(1-s)/\zeta(1-s)$ after its pole at $1$ is removed.
The added power has the exact transform
\[
  \int_X^\infty x^{s-b-1}\,dx=\frac{X^{s-b}}{b-s}
  \qquad(\Re s<b).
\]
These formulas define an analytic continuation $H_b(s)$ wherever the zeta
factor is regular.

Here is the transform calculation used in that sentence.  The Frullani
identity
\[
 k(t)=\int_0^\infty (u+1)t^{-(u+2)}\,du
\]
and Fubini's theorem give, for $\Re s<0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-mellin-explicit}
 \int_3^\infty x^{s-1}J(x)\,dx
 =\int_0^\infty (u+1)
 \frac{\Phi_3(u+1-s)-3^s\Phi_3(u+1)}{s}\,du,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \Phi_3(z)={}&
 \left(\frac{-\zeta'(z)}{z\zeta(z)}-\frac1{z-1}\right)\notag\\
 &-\int_1^3(\psi(t)-t)t^{-z-1}\,dt.
 \label{eq:psi-tail-continuation}
\end{align}
The apparent value at $s=0$ is filled by analytic continuation.  Absolute
integrability of the three-variable carrier $3<x<t$, $u>0$ licenses the two
exchanges of integration in \eqref{eq:J-mellin-explicit}; compact and
large-$u$ majorants then prove analyticity of the filled expression.  Formula
\eqref{eq:psi-tail-continuation} makes the singularity source explicit: the
finite startup integral is entire, while the logarithmic derivative records
the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$.

We now separate the two roles of Landau's argument.  First, positivity of
$\mu_b$ makes the real convergence set of \eqref{eq:landau-mgf} convex.
At an interior point $a$, the derivatives of $M_b$ are its nonnegative
moments
\[
  \int (\log x)^k e^{a\log x}\,d\mu_b(x).
\]
If an analytic continuation has a power-series radius reaching $a+d$, its
Taylor series bounds the sum of these moments at $d\ge0$; monotone
convergence then proves actual integrability at $a+d$.  Iterating this strict
crossing shows that every real $\sigma<b$ lies in the interior of the true
integrability set.  Consequently $M_b$ itself is analytic throughout the
half-plane $\Re s<b$ and agrees there with $H_b$ by the identity theorem.

Second, put $s_0=1-\rho_*$.  Since
$\Re s_0=1-\Re\rho_*<b$, the preceding conclusion makes $M_b$ analytic, and
therefore locally bounded, at $s_0$.  Approach that point along
\[
  s_\delta=1-\rho_*-\delta,\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Maximality of $\Re\rho_*$ on its horizontal line says that
$\zeta(\rho_*+\delta)\ne0$, so $H_b(s_\delta)$ is regular for every
$\delta>0$.  At the endpoint, however, $1-s_0=\rho_*$ and the logarithmic
derivative has the zero pole; the norm of $H_b(s_\delta)$ tends to infinity
as $\delta\downarrow0$.  Since $M_b(s_\delta)=H_b(s_\delta)$, analytic
boundedness and divergence contradict one another.  Thus the assumed
eventual positivity is impossible, proving \eqref{eq:J-omega}.
\end{proof}

The proof just given is the strict form of the oscillation argument used by
Nicolas and Robin.  Its quantifiers are important: the excursions occur
arbitrarily far out, rather than at one selected numerical frontier.
The positive-transform step is not imported as an unnamed principle.
Its reusable formal statement proves that if a nonnegative exponential
transform converges at every real exponent below a frontier and has an
analytic continuation across the frontier, then it actually converges at
some strictly larger exponent.  The proof expands the continuation into its
power series, identifies its coefficients with nonnegative moments, and
uses monotone convergence to recover exponential integrability.  This
generic theorem is linked explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:formalization};
the subsequent Nicolas modules supply \eqref{eq:J-mellin-explicit}, the
rightmost-zero ray, and the blow-up contradiction.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:finite-nicolas-omega}
If $\RH$ is false, there exists $0<b<1/2$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logF-omega}
  \log F(x)=\Omega_-(x^{-b}).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:nicolas-comparison} and
$x^{-1}=o(x^{-b})$, which holds because $b<1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Least common multiples and the converse}\label{sec:lcm}

Let
\[
  L_P=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,P).
\]
The classical identity $\log L_P=\psi(P)$ connects this finite integer with
the analytic frontier.  Define the logarithmic height loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height-loss}
  D(P)=\log\log\psi(P)-\log\log\vartheta(P).
\end{equation}
The saturated Euler product differs from the actual abundancy of $L_P$ by
the nonnegative prime-power reserve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tower-reserve}
  T(P)=\sum_{p\le P}
   -\log\!\left(1-p^{-a_p-1}\right),
  \qquad
  a_p=\max\{a:p^a\le P\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact LCM identity]\label{lem:lcm-identity}
For all sufficiently large $P$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-identity}
  \M(L_P)=-\log F(P)-D(P)-T(P).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The finite Euler product gives
\[
  \sum_{p\le P}-\log(1-p^{-1})
   =-\log \Pprod(P),
\]
where $\Pprod(P)$ denotes the product in \eqref{eq:nicolas-function}.  The
prime-power factorization of $L_P$ gives
\[
  -\log \Pprod(P)=\log\A(L_P)+T(P).
\]
Now expand $\log F(P)$ and use $\log L_P=\psi(P)$.  Rearrangement is exactly
\eqref{eq:lcm-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The transfer losses]\label{lem:transfer-loss}
For every $b<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss-little-o}
  D(P)+T(P)=o(P^{-b}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The elementary bound
\[
  0\le\psi(P)-\vartheta(P)
  \le2\sqrt P\log P
\]
and a linear lower bound for $\vartheta(P)$ give
\[
  0\le D(P)\ll \frac{\log P}{\sqrt P}.
\]
Splitting the reserve at $\sqrt P$ and summing the geometric tails gives
$T(P)\ll P^{-1/2}$.  Since
$P^{-1/2}\log P=o(P^{-b})$ whenever $b<1/2$, the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The converse implication]\label{thm:converse}
If Robin's inequality holds for every $n>5040$, then $\RH$ holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\RH$ is false.  Choose $b$ as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-nicolas-omega}.  Negative excursions of
$\log F(P)$ give positive excursions of $-\log F(P)$ of order $P^{-b}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-loss}, subtracting $D(P)+T(P)$ preserves such
excursions.  Lemma~\ref{lem:lcm-identity} therefore gives arbitrarily large
$P$ with $\M(L_P)>0$.  Since $L_P\ge P$, these least common multiples
eventually exceed $5040$, and $\M(L_P)>0$ is a literal failure of Robin's
inequality.  This contradicts the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\section{Lagarias's elementary criterion}\label{sec:lagarias}

Write $H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n 1/k$.  Lagarias proved the following elementary
reformulation of the Riemann hypothesis~\cite{Lagarias2002}.

\begin{theorem}[Lagarias]\label{thm:lagarias}
The Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if every positive integer $n$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagarias}
  \sig(n)\le H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The formal proof deliberately reuses the hard Robin work rather than
reconstructing it.  For the forward implication, Lagarias's Lemma~3.1 gives,
for $n\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagarias-lower}
  e^{\euler}n\log\log n\le e^{H_n}\log H_n.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\euler+\log n\le H_n$, exponentiation gives
$e^{\euler}n\le e^{H_n}$, and
$\log n\le H_n$ gives $\log\log n\le\log H_n$; all factors have the
required signs.  Under $\RH$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
\eqref{eq:lagarias-lower} prove \eqref{eq:lagarias} for $n>5040$.
The case $n=1$ is equality.  The interval $2\le n\le5040$ is split into
46 consecutive blocks.  Each block records an exact upper bound for
$\sig(n)$ throughout the block and rational lower bounds for $H_n$,
$e^{H_n}$, and $\log H_n$.  The reusable block soundness theorem converts
those checks into \eqref{eq:lagarias}; all arithmetic checks use
\path{decide +kernel}, not \path{native_decide}.

For the converse, a relaxed form of Lagarias's Lemma~3.2 suffices:
for $n\ge20$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagarias-upper}
 H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n
 \le e^{\euler}n\log\log n+\frac{24n}{\log n}.
\end{equation}
The published lemma has the sharper constant $7$; no sharp constant is
needed here.  Apply \eqref{eq:lagarias-upper} to
$L_P=\mathop{\rm lcm}(1,\ldots,P)$.  If \eqref{eq:lagarias} holds, the
positive logarithmic Robin margin at $L_P$ is at most
$24/\log L_P$.  The elementary Chebyshev lower bound
$\log L_P\gg P$ makes this $O(P^{-1})$.  The already formalized
Nicolas--Landau argument under $\neg\RH$ supplies positive margin excursions
of order $P^{-b}$ for some $0<b<1/2$, while $P^{-1}=o(P^{-b})$.
The two estimates contradict one another for arbitrarily large $P$.

The corresponding Lean declarations are

\section{Checked specializations and the Hecke construction}
\label{app:hecke-formalization}

Degree one includes unitary Hecke $L$-functions. For the trivial
Hecke character of a field $K$, Lean verifies that the triangular
bound is equivalent to Dedekind RH and to the corrected ideal
criterion for that field. Both proofs are in
\path{Robin1984/GRH/DedekindTriangular.lean}.
For a unitary Hecke character $\chi$, Lean constructs its local
parameters $\alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)$ from the finite ideles, with
$\alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)=0$ at ramified primes. It proves the Euler
product on $\Re s>1$ and the paired coefficient
$b_{\mathfrak p,j}=2\Re(\alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)^j)\log N\mathfrak p$
for every $j\ge1$ in \path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeEulerProduct.lean}.
The same file evaluates the finite local Tate integrals on $\Re s>0$,
using multiplicative Haar measure normalized by the unit subgroup.
For each $t\in\mathbb R$ the unitary idelic norm character
$\chi_t=|\cdot|^{it}$ gives $L(s,\chi_t)=\zeta_K(s+it)$ on $\Re s>1$.
Lean constructs its shifted continuation and proves that its critical-line
property is equivalent to both the corrected ideal Robin criterion and the
triangular prime-power bound for $K$.
The local exponents give the conductor ideal in
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeConductor.lean}. The real and complex
shifts and their radial Gaussian Mellin evaluations on $\Re s>0$
are verified in \path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeArchimedean.lean}.
The resulting Hecke triangular criterion uses those local data.

\paragraph{The adelic Poisson formula for a finite coset.}
Lean verifies that the diagonal is discrete and that
$\mathbb A_K/K$ is compact. It constructs the continuous additive
character $\psi$ that is trivial on the diagonal and proves that the
finite character annihilator for $d\widehat{\mathcal O}_K$ is the
trace-dual ideal $(d)^\vee$. With mass one on
$\widehat{\mathcal O}_K$, the finite additive Haar measure satisfies
$N((d))\mu_f(d\widehat{\mathcal O}_K)=1$.

For every $a\in\mathbb A_{K,f}$, $0\ne d\in\mathcal O_K$, and Schwartz
function $f$ on the weighted Minkowski space,
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeAdelePoisson.lean} proves
\[
  \sum_{\substack{x\in K\\ x_f\in a+d\widehat{\mathcal O}_K}} f(e_K(x))
  =
  \frac{1}{N((d))\sqrt{|D_K|}}
  \sum_{\xi\in(d)^\vee} \psi_f(-a\xi)\widehat f(c_K(e_K(\xi))).
\]
Here $x_f$ is the finite component of the principal adele, $e_K$ weights
the complex coordinates by $\sqrt{2}$, and $c_K$ conjugates those coordinates.

The finite Tate test $T_\chi$ is the product of the integral-coordinate
indicators at unramified places and the inverse local character on the unit
group at ramified places, extended by zero there off the units.
For every $b\in\mathbb A_{K,f}^{\times}$, clearing a principal denominator
reduces $z\mapsto T_\chi(zb)$ to a function supported on
$\widehat{\mathcal O}_K$ and constant modulo a nonzero principal ideal.
Pulling the finite residue-class expansion back through the rescaling
gives a finite sum of principal coset indicators.
For every such $b$ and every archimedean Schwartz function $f$,
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeFiniteTateTest.lean} proves
\[
  \sum_{x\in K} f(e_K(x))T_\chi(x_f b)
  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}\sum_{\xi\in K}
    \widehat f(c_K(e_K(\xi)))
    \int_{\mathbb A_{K,f}} T_\chi(zb)\psi_f(-\xi_f z)\,d\mu_f(z).
\]
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeFiniteHaarDilation.lean} identifies the
additive Haar modulus of a finite idele with its finite idelic norm.
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeAdelicFourier.lean} applies the resulting
change of variables together with the archimedean Fourier scaling
to prove the Poisson formula with both idele components dilated.

For the multiplicative quotient,
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeFiniteIdealClass.lean} proves the
surjective finite-idele map onto nonzero fractional ideals and
identifies its compact kernel as the integral finite units.
Using Dirichlet's unit theorem and the finiteness of the ideal
class group, \path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeIdeleClassCompactness.lean}
constructs a compact set of norm-one ideles meeting every
norm-one idele class. It also proves that the principal
subgroup is closed and discrete. On those compact representatives,
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeTateThetaDecay.lean} proves an absolute
theta-sum estimate by clearing one common finite denominator and
then summing the archimedean Schwartz bound over a lattice.
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeTateDualSupport.lean} verifies compact support
and boundedness for the finite Fourier dual.
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeTateThetaQuotient.lean} descends the sum with
its zero term removed to the norm-one class quotient. It proves
uniform exponential decay in the logarithmic norm parameter for
the original and dual tests. These are the large-norm estimates
used in Tate's Mellin argument~\cite{Tate1967}.
\path{Robin1984/GRH/HeckeTateMellin.lean} proves joint continuity on
the norm-one class quotient and absolute Mellin integrability at
every real exponent for both tests. A unitary Hecke character
descends to this quotient. Its integral against invariant measure
vanishes if the restriction is nontrivial. The module also proves
that the character-weighted positive tail defines an entire function
of the Mellin parameter. The module
\path{HeckeTateQuotientIntegration.lean} proves the product formula
for the full quotient measure using a Borel fundamental domain.
The module \path{HeckeTateZetaUnfolding.lean} identifies the
principal-idele orbit sum with the actual theta series and unfolds
the weighted zeta integral. For the canonical polynomial Gaussian and
finite Tate test, \path{HeckeTateAdelicEuler.lean} proves its equality
on \(\Re s>1\) with the Gamma factors times the Hecke Euler product.
The quotient Poisson identity in \path{HeckeTateClassPoisson.lean}
keeps the two terms at zero. Averaging against a character nontrivial
on norm-one idele classes makes both terms vanish.

\paragraph{The conductor in the functional equation.}
Write \(Q_\chi=|D_K|N\mathfrak f_\chi\). Choose finite ideles \(d,c\)
representing the different and conductor ideals and put \(b=dc\).
\path{HeckeTateFiniteFourier.lean} proves
\[
   T_\chi^{\mathrm D}(y)
      =T_\chi^{\mathrm D}(b^{-1})T_{\chi^{-1}}(by),
   \qquad |b|_f=Q_\chi^{-1},
\]
where \(T_\chi^{\mathrm D}\) is the finite Fourier test with the
discriminant normalization and trace sign of the Poisson formula.
To prove the first identity, trace duality identifies the annihilator
of the integral adeles with the inverse different. Translation by
the conductor forces \(by\) to be integral on the support of the
transform. At every ramified place, covariance under local units
forces \(by\) to be a unit. On that support one can match the
ramified coordinates by an integral finite idele, and covariance
determines the transform from its value at \(b^{-1}\).
\path{HeckeTateInfiniteFourier.lean} computes the archimedean
Fourier test as \(P_\chi f_{\chi^{-1}}\), where
\[
  P_\chi=(-i)^{\sum_{v\ \mathrm{real}}\epsilon_v
                       +\sum_{v\ \mathrm{complex}}|m_v|}
\]
for the real parities \(\epsilon_v\) and complex angular weights \(m_v\).

Let \(a(t)\) be the positive archimedean section of norm \(e^t\)
with finite component one, and let \(G_\chi(t)\) be the canonical
theta sum averaged against \(\chi\) on the norm-one class group.
With \(\beta=\log|b|_f=-\log Q_\chi\), the idele \(a(-\beta)b\)
has norm one. Haar invariance under multiplication by its class \(w\)
gives the shifted identity
\[
  G_\chi(t)=k_\chi e^{-t}G_{\chi^{-1}}(\beta-t),\qquad
  k_\chi=P_\chi T_\chi^{\mathrm D}(b^{-1})\chi^{-1}(w^{-1}).
\]
The finite translation changes the logarithmic norm by \(\beta\).
The factor \(\chi^{-1}(w^{-1})\) comes from translating the
character in the averaging integral.

\paragraph{The completed family.}
Write \(\chi(a(t))=e^{i\tau_\chi t}\) with \(\tau_\chi\in\mathbb R\).
The entire completion is
\[
  \Lambda_\chi(s)=e^{-s\beta/2}
      \int_{\mathbb R}e^{st}\chi(a(t))G_\chi(t)\,dt .
\]
Fubini's theorem and reflection express the full integral as
two positive Mellin tails at \(s+i\tau_\chi\) and
\(1-s-i\tau_\chi\). The absolute moments for all real exponents
justify both tails and their derivatives. On \(\Re s>1\), the
unfolded Euler integral identifies \(\Lambda_\chi\) with
\(Q_\chi^{s/2}\) times the Gamma factors and \(L(s,\chi)\).
Changing variables \(u=\beta-t\) in the full integral gives
\[
  \Lambda_\chi(s)=\omega_\chi\Lambda_{\chi^{-1}}(1-s),\qquad
  \omega_\chi=k_\chi\exp(-\beta/2+i\tau_\chi\beta).
\]
The canonical tests and norm-section character for \(\chi^{-1}\)
are the complex conjugates of those for \(\chi\). Consequently,
\(\Lambda_{\chi^{-1}}(\bar s)=\overline{\Lambda_\chi(s)}\).
Applying the reflected conjugate identity twice gives
\(\Lambda_\chi(s)=|\omega_\chi|^2\Lambda_\chi(s)\).
The completed Euler product is nonzero for \(\Re s>1\), so
\(|\omega_\chi|=1\).

On \(-2\le\Re s\le3\) the moments at exponent \(3\) bound both
tails, and the conductor exponential is bounded by
\(\exp(3|\beta|/2)\). The paired product has order at most one by
the Euler and Gamma bounds and the completed functional equation.
The paired triangular criterion therefore applies without an
additional analytic premise for these characters. If the restriction
to norm-one classes is trivial, the character is determined by
its values on \(a(t)\) and is a norm twist. The pole-cleared
completion is then the translated Dedekind completion, for which
the Dedekind triangular criterion applies. The full degree-one
result is in \path{HeckeTateFunctionalEquation.lean}.

A weighted all-ideal bound can also be obtained when the
logarithmic Euler coefficients are nonnegative. For unitary Hecke
characters other than pure norm twists, the coefficients of
$\zeta_K(s)^2L(s,\chi)L(s,\bar\chi)$ divided by $\log N\mathfrak p$
are $2+2\Re(\alpha_\chi(\mathfrak p)^j)\ge0$ for every $j\ge1$.
Subtracting a price $1/(x\log x)$ for each unit of weighted log norm
then cancels the Mertens boundary term exactly. The details of the
weighted comparison are recorded in \path{docs/GRH.md}.

\subsection{How the checked artifact was produced}

What the final proof says and how it was produced are different questions.
The former is answered by the declarations above and by the mathematical
proof in Sections~\ref{sec:extremal}--\ref{sec:lcm}.  The production process
was a source-guided reconstruction with the following stages.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=.3em]
  \item The public statements were fixed first in
    \codelink{Challenge.lean}{Challenge}, directly over Mathlib definitions
    and without importing any project module.  This prevented later proof
    engineering from silently changing the target.
  \item Robin's argument and the cited inputs from Nicolas, Landau, Alaoglu--
    Erd\H{o}s, Rosser--Schoenfeld, and Costa Pereira were decomposed into named
    intermediate statements.  Source-specific results remained in their
    mathematical families, while reusable order, integration, logarithm,
    Mellin-transform, and prime-power lemmas were separated from the final
    theorem assembly.
  \item Analytic identities were proved symbolically before numerical
    constants were introduced.  Infinite sums and integrals were accompanied
    by explicit summability or integrability proofs; the weighted zero sums
    retained analytic multiplicity.
  \item The finite-range checker and its soundness theorem were proved before
    certificate data were accepted.  Candidate rows and prime blocks could be
    found by ordinary computation, but only their exact integer and rational
    outputs entered Lean, where kernel reduction checked them.
  \item The resulting source graph was reduced to the declarations needed by
    the advertised results and the public convenience facades.  Direct-import,
    provenance, placeholder, metadata, statement-boundary, and independent
    replay checks were then applied to the completed graph.
\end{enumerate}
Human direction selected the theorem statements, mathematical route, source
correspondences, and final assertions.  AI-assisted proof engineering helped
with implementation, refactoring, certificate organization, and audits.  The
production history is not itself a proof: correctness rests on the resulting
proof terms, exact certificates, and the validation pipeline described below.

\subsection{Automated research and formalization context system}

The AI assistance was not a single prompt followed by unchecked code
generation.  The project used a persistent, structured context system that
connected four kinds of state:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=.3em]
  \item \emph{Research state} associated claims with exact source passages,
    bibliographic identities, chronology, and provenance classifications.
    Later reconstructions and formalization choices were kept distinct from
    results stated by Robin, Nicolas, Landau, and the other cited authors.
  \item \emph{Mathematical state} recorded exact definitions, quantifier
    signatures, sign conventions, normalizations, numerical domains, and the
    current hard lemma connecting an open branch to the advertised theorem.
    Numerical experiments were attached only as evidence for a matching
    domain, never promoted to hypotheses.
  \item \emph{Formal state} tracked the exact Lean declaration under attack,
    its direct and transitive dependencies, import providers, proof-state
    obligations, prior failed approaches, focused-build evidence, and the
    declarations on the final dependency path.
  \item \emph{Assurance state} tracked placeholder and axiom audits, provenance
    coverage, direct-import checks, certificate coverage, statement-boundary
    comparison, kernel replay, and release status.  Substantive mistakes were
    converted into explicit preflight rules before the same class of action
    could be repeated.
\end{enumerate}

Reusable task-specific \emph{skills} formed the control layer over that
state.  A skill is a versioned workflow package activated for a class of
work, not a learned mathematical oracle.  The packages used here covered:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=.25em]
  \item source and provenance review, including chronology and the distinction
    between direct source formalization and project-original glue;
  \item hard-theorem discipline, which fixes the live declaration, forbids
    replacing a universal parameter by an unjustified finite experiment, and
    separates proved results from certificate evidence and open obligations;
  \item numerical and certificate work, requiring the proposed inequality and
    its domain to be checked independently before Lean formalization and
    requiring every retained certificate to pass a general soundness theorem;
  \item repository engineering, including exact import providers, dependency
    closure, placeholder scans, focused builds, and staged-surface checks; and
  \item paper and artifact production, requiring source-to-declaration
    correspondence, exact-link checks, repeated PDF builds, extracted-text
    checks, and page-by-page visual inspection.
\end{itemize}
The context router activated the smallest applicable set and supplied each
skill with the same live theorem identity and evidence ledger.  Skills could
compose---for example, a numerical bound proposed during proof work could not
enter the paper until the numerical-domain rule, Lean proof rule, provenance
rule, and document-verification rule all agreed on its status.  Their durable
preflight and hard-stop conditions also carried lessons from a failed build
or incorrect claim into later sessions, instead of relying on conversational
memory alone.

The skill library also evolved during the project.  When a build failure,
mathematical contradiction, inaccurate claim, or user correction exposed a
substantive failure class, work on the affected path stopped.  The system
recorded four fields: the triggering condition, a check that must run before
the action is attempted again, the action now prohibited, and the corrected
action.  A repeated failure strengthened the entry with a mechanically tested
hard stop.  Rules that applied only to one declaration remained in the
project context; rules that generalized across formalizations were promoted
into, or used to revise, a reusable skill.  Subsequent sessions loaded the
revised package before acting, so failure-derived knowledge changed future
behavior even when the earlier conversation was no longer present.

These examples expose the task inputs, the accepted decomposition, the human
acceptance criterion, the retained source output, and the recurrence barrier.
They are the two documented workflow cases evaluated here, not a complete
telemetry record of every exploratory interaction.  No baseline run without
the system was performed, so the paper makes no causal claim that automation
reduced elapsed time, human effort, or the total number of errors.  The public
before-and-after commits, exact proof terms, check scripts, and CI logs make
the narrower claims about retained outputs and recurrence barriers testable.

This evolution is controlled rather than autonomous self-modification of the
proof environment.  Skill revisions are explicit, reviewable instruction
artifacts; they are checked on representative tasks and remain subordinate to
human direction and repository policy.  They may alter search order,
preflight checks, context selection, or stopping conditions, but they cannot
alter a theorem statement, convert evidence into a proof, whitelist a new
axiom, or bypass Lean's kernel and the independent replay gates.  The public
artifact therefore depends on the skills for engineering discipline, not for
logical soundness.

At each stage, the system selected only the source excerpts, definitions,
theorem types, dependency fragments, and earlier failure evidence relevant to
the live obligation.  This constrained context mattered more than raw context
volume: it reduced namespace confusion, accidental quantifier changes,
unjustified numerical specialization, duplicate lemmas, and work on
declarations outside the final proof graph.  Candidate formal steps were
tested first against the smallest relevant module; expensive full builds were
run only for coherent repository states.

Human control remained at the mathematical boundary.  The maintainer chose
the target theorems, sources, proof route, attribution, and public claims.
Automated agents did propose intermediate definitions and lemma decompositions,
search library interfaces, construct candidate proof terms, generate exact
certificate candidates, refactor dependencies, and run audits.  Human review
selected which definitions, statements, and proofs were retained.  Every
accepted output is visible as Lean source, rational data, provenance metadata,
or a validation log; no model response, research summary, numerical search,
confidence score, or context record is trusted by either advertised theorem.

The methodological claim is therefore limited to the architecture and the two
cases above.  Reproducing the checked result requires the public source and
certificates, not a replay of the exploratory interaction history: the build,
Comparator, and two kernel implementations check the retained artifact.

\subsection{What Comparator and the kernel replays establish}

The validation stages have different jobs.  The ordinary Lean build checks
that every project module elaborates and that Lean's kernel accepts the final
proof terms.
\href{https://github.com/leanprover/comparator/tree/68a064109f01c08f47c8edc9f51d6a2bbffaa188}
{Comparator} then protects the public theorem boundary against a different
risk: a purported solution might prove an easier proposition with the same
informal description, or redefine a symbol occurring in the target.

The \codelink{Challenge.lean}{Challenge} module imports only Mathlib. Its
classical declarations state Robin's criterion, the CA reduction and Lagarias's
criterion. The module also states the Dirichlet GRH and Dedekind ERH ideal
criteria and the triangular criterion for the analytic data of grand RH.
All Challenge theorems have deliberate proof holes.
The \codelink{Solution.lean}{Solution} module imports the project and supplies
the proofs. The \codelink{comparator.json}{Comparator configuration} names the
three classical and two corrected ideal declarations and permits only
\leanname{propext}, \leanname{Quot.sound}, and
\leanname{Classical.choice}, requires NanoDa, and declares no definition
holes.  In particular, the solution is not allowed to choose a convenient
meaning for $\sig$, Robin's inequality, CA, or $\RH$.

The complete semantic policy is short enough to display:
\begin{verbatim}
"theorem_names": [
  "Robin1984.robin_inequality_iff_riemannHypothesis",
  "Robin1984.riemannHypothesis_iff_colossallyAbundant_robin",
  "Robin1984.riemann_hypothesis_iff_lagarias_elementary_criterion",
  "Robin1984.GRH.dirichletGRH_iff_cyclotomicCorrectedIdealRobin",
  "Robin1984.GRH.dedekindERH_iff_correctedIdealRobin"
],
"definition_names": [],
"permitted_axioms": ["propext", "Quot.sound", "Classical.choice"],
"enable_nanoda": true
\end{verbatim}
The omitted JSON fields only name the modules \path{Challenge} and
\path{Solution}; there is no additional whitelist of project definitions.

Using pinned tool revisions, the Comparator job performs the following checks.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=.3em]
  \item It builds and exports the Challenge and Solution environments
    separately under the Landrun sandbox.
  \item For each advertised theorem it compares the exported statement and
    every declaration on which that statement depends.  This is a comparison
    of Lean declarations, not of displayed text or theorem names alone.
  \item It follows the Solution theorem bodies transitively and rejects any
    axiom outside the three permitted standard principles.
  \item It replays the exported Solution environment through Lean's default
    kernel.
  \item Because \leanname{enable\_nanoda} is true, it also submits the export
    to NanoDa, an independent kernel implementation.
\end{enumerate}
Passing Comparator therefore establishes statement identity, the configured
axiom bound, and kernel acceptance through two implementations.  It does not
establish that the trusted Challenge statement is the historically intended
reading of Robin's paper, nor that the exposition is persuasive.  The explicit
Challenge definitions, the expansion of Mathlib's $\RH$ predicate above, and
ordinary mathematical review address that separate question.

\section{Exact rational coefficient audit}\label{app:coefficients}

This appendix collects the arithmetic intentionally omitted from the main
narrative of Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-gap}.  First, the formal bound
for the zero constant uses
\[
 \euler<H_{256}-\log256,\qquad
 H_{256}\le\frac{15311}{2500},\qquad
 \log2\ge\frac{69314}{100000},\qquad
 \log(4\pi)\ge\frac{253}{100}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 c_0<
 \frac{15311}{2500}-8\frac{69314}{100000}
 +2-\frac{253}{100}
 =\frac{154}{3125}<\frac1{20}.
\]
This is the rational proof behind the decimal orientation
$c_0=0.0461914179\ldots$.

For the error bound, put $q=(\log H)^{-1}$.  The
$H^{-1/6}$ estimate in \eqref{eq:error-rational-data} turns
$2H^{-1/6}$ into at most $(87/25)q$.  Combining this with the favorable
term $-2q$ and applying the remaining bounds gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\E(H)}{S(H)}
 &\le
 2+\frac{37}{25}\frac5{56}
 +8\left(\frac5{56}\right)^2\\
 &\quad+\frac1{20}
   \left(1+\frac5{56}+4\left(\frac5{56}\right)^2\right)
 +\frac{46}{25}\frac1{272}\\
 &=\frac{3010451}{1332800}
 =\frac{113}{50}-\frac{1677}{1332800}
 <\frac{113}{50}.
\end{align*}

For the complete prime cost, Abel summation over the prime-square block
$\sqrt{2H}<p\le H/2$ gives the following normalized lower expression:
\begin{align*}
 &2\sqrt2\frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}
    \left(1-\frac1{\log H+\log2}\right)\\
 &\quad-\frac94\,2^{1/4}H^{-1/4}
    \frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}
 -\log(2\pi)H^{-1/2}
    \frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}\\
 &\quad-4H^{-1/2}\frac{\log H}{\log H-\log2}\\
 &\quad-2\sqrt2\,c_0H^{-1}\frac{\log H}{\log H-\log2}
    \left(2+\frac1{\log H-\log2}
      +\frac4{3(\log H-\log2)^2}\right).
\end{align*}
The last line is the far-end RH term retained through the cancellation.
The rational bounds used for $H\ge74500$ are
\begin{align}\label{eq:cost-rational-data}
 \sqrt2&\ge\frac{7071}{5000}, & 2^{1/4}&\le\frac{119}{100},\notag\\
 \frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}&\ge\frac{16}{17}, &
 \frac1{\log H+\log2}&\le\frac{17}{202},\notag\\
 H^{-1/4}\frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}&\le\frac{573}{10000}, &
 H^{-1/2}\frac{\log H}{\log H+\log2}&\le\frac7{2000},\notag\\
 H^{-1/2}\frac{\log H}{\log H-\log2}&\le\frac{197}{50000}, &
 \frac{\log H}{\log H-\log2}&\le\frac{107}{100}.
\end{align}
The normalized far-end term is at most $1/10000$, so the final exact
substitution is
\begin{align*}
 &2\frac{7071}{5000}\frac{16}{17}
    \left(1-\frac{17}{202}\right)
 -\frac94\frac{119}{100}\frac{573}{10000}
 -\frac{46}{25}\frac7{2000}\\
 &\hspace{2em}-4\frac{197}{50000}-\frac1{10000}\\
 &\qquad=
 \frac{15537277889}{6868000000}
 =\frac{113}{50}+\frac{15597889}{6868000000}
 >\frac{113}{50}.
\end{align*}
